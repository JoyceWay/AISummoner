NIGHT 01 · 第一夜

CHAPTER 1 · THE REACH OF EXPLANATIONS解释的 reach

Tonight answers just one question: what does a good explanation look like? The answer hides in a goddess and a tilted axis.

今夜只回答一个问题：好解释长什么样？答案藏在一个女神和一根倾斜的地轴里。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGNo one has been to a star没有人去过星星

No one has ever been to a star. No one has touched one, no one has drilled into one to look inside.

没有人去过一颗星星。没有人摸过它，没有人钻进它的内部看一眼。

And yet astronomers know why stars shine: nuclear fusion. Hydrogen becomes helium, mass becomes light.

可天文学家知道星星为什么发光：核聚变。氢变成氦，质量变成光。

How do they know? Not by looking harder — the senses can't reach there. They know because they have an explanation that fits.

他们是怎么知道的？不是靠"看得更仔细"——感官到不了那里。靠的是解释。

This is the starting point of the whole book, and Deutsch's reversal of common sense: knowledge doesn't get "read out" of the senses. Empiricism says theories come from sense reports — we see things, then generalize. Deutsch says no. Theories are guessed, then criticized, picked apart, replaced. The senses are the judge, not the author.

这就是整本书的起点，也是 Deutsch 对常识的一次翻转：知识不是从感官里"读"出来的。经验主义说，理论来自感官报告——我们看到什么，就总结出什么。Deutsch 说，不对。理论是猜出来的，然后被批评、被挑刺、被改掉。感官只是裁判，不是作者。

So the question becomes: how do you tell a good guess from a bad one?

所以问题变成：猜出来的东西，怎么分好坏？

SCENE 2 · THE STORYThe goddess's winter女神的冬天

Long ago, the Greeks also had to explain winter. They told this story:

很久以前，希腊人也要解释冬天。他们讲了这样一个故事：

Hades, king of the underworld, kidnapped Persephone, goddess of spring. Her mother Demeter, goddess of the earth, grieved, and negotiated: her daughter must return to the underworld for part of each year. And in the days when her daughter was gone, Demeter's sadness made the earth cold — that was winter.

冥王哈迪斯绑走了春之女神珀耳塞福涅。她的母亲、地母得墨忒耳悲痛欲绝，与哈迪斯谈判：女儿每年必须回冥界住一段时间。而在女儿离开的日子里，得墨忒耳的悲伤让大地变冷——那就是冬天。

Is the story beautiful? Yes. Does it explain winter? It seems to.

这个故事美吗？美。能解释冬天吗？好像能。

And — here's the key — the story is testable. If winter is caused by Demeter's grief, winter should fall on the whole earth at once. But Australians will tell you: in December, it's summer there. The story is refuted.

而且——关键来了——这个故事是可以被检验的。如果冬天是得墨忒耳的悲伤造成的，那冬天应该同时降临整个大地。可澳大利亚人会告诉你：十二月，他们那里是夏天。故事被证伪了。

So is "testable" the standard of a good explanation? Deutsch says: no. That's the real turn of the first night.

所以"可检验"就是好解释的标准吗？Deutsch 说：不是。这才是第一夜真正的转折。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNA story fixed with one sentence一句话就能修好的故事

Suppose a Greek heard about summer in the southern hemisphere. Would he have to abandon the myth? No. One sentence fixes it:

假设一个希腊人听说了南半球的夏天。他需要抛弃这个神话吗？不需要。他一句话就能修好它：

"When Demeter is sad she banishes warmth from her vicinity, and it has to go elsewhere — into the southern hemisphere."
得墨忒耳悲伤时，把温暖从她身边驱逐出去——温暖无处可去，只好跑到南半球。

Fixed. Winter in the north, summer in the south, the story still works. Swap the god, swap the motive, add a clause — change it any way you like.

修好了。冬天在北半球，夏天在南半球，故事依然成立。换一个神、换一个动机、加一个条款——怎么改都行。

That is the true face of a bad explanation: easy to vary. Deutsch's definition at the end of the chapter:

这就是坏解释的真面目：easy to vary（容易变通）。Deutsch 在章末的定义是：

A bad explanation is one "that is easy to vary while still accounting for what it purports to account for."
坏解释就是那种"随便改改，还能解释它要解释的东西"的解释。

An explanation that can explain anything actually explains nothing. If it can explain green rainbows, weekly seasons, or no seasons at all — then it hasn't explained winter.

一个解释如果能解释任何东西，那它其实什么都没解释。能解释绿色彩虹、每周一次的四季、或者根本没有四季——那它就没有解释冬天。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTThe tilted axis倾斜的地轴

Now the real explanation: the Earth's axis is tilted. As the Earth orbits the sun, each hemisphere leans toward the sun for half the year and away for the other half. Leaning toward is summer; leaning away is winter.

现在看真正的解释：地轴是倾斜的。地球绕太阳转时，每个半球有半年朝向太阳，半年背向太阳。朝向太阳就是夏天，背向就是冬天。

Try changing it. Swap the sun for the moon? No — the moon's position in the sky doesn't repeat once a year, while the seasons do. Remove "tilted surfaces receive less heat"? No — the whole explanation collapses. Remove the tilt? Then there are no seasons.

试着改它。把太阳换成月亮？不行——月亮在天空中的位置一年不重复一次，而四季是一年一循环。把"倾斜的表面受热更少"这句去掉？不行——那整个解释就塌了。把倾斜改成不倾斜？那就没有四季了。

Every detail is doing work; change one, and the explanation stops explaining. That is hard to vary:

每一个细节都在干活，改一个，解释就停止解释。这就是 hard to vary（很难变通）：

That is a good explanation — hard to vary, because all its details play a functional role.
这就是好解释——很难变通，因为它的每一个细节都在起功能作用。

And these details are known independently: we can verify in a lab, without waiting for winter, that a tilted surface is heated less; we know without watching the seasons that a spinning sphere in space points in a constant direction. The parts of the explanation come from elsewhere; assembled, they happen to explain the seasons.

而且这些细节是独立知道的：我们不用等冬天就能在实验室里验证"倾斜的表面受热更少"，不用看四季就知道"旋转的球体在空间中指向恒定"。解释的零件来自别处，拼在一起，恰好解释了四季。

By the way: precisely because this explanation is hard to vary, it takes a risk. If December in England had the same weather as December in Australia, the whole theory would have to be thrown out — no small repairs allowed. Good explanations put themselves in danger like that.

顺便说一句：正因为这个解释很难变通，所以它冒着风险。如果十二月的英格兰和十二月的澳大利亚天气一样，这个理论就得整个扔掉，不能小修小补。好解释就是这样——它把自己置于危险之中。

SCENE 5 · THE RISEReach: an explanation travels farther than its birthplaceReach：解释走得比出生地更远

The tilted axis doesn't just explain Earth's seasons. It predicts other things: the southern hemisphere's seasons must be opposite the northern; Mars (axial tilt about 25°, close to Earth's 23.5°) must have seasons too; the poles must have half a year of day and half a year of night.

地轴倾斜不只解释了地球的四季。它还预测了别的东西：南半球的四季必须和北半球相反；火星（地轴倾角约 25°，和地球的 23.5° 接近）也必须有四季；两极必须有半年白天、半年黑夜。

None of these were problems the explanation was "born" to solve. It was born to explain Earth's winter, and it went on to explain Mars and predict the southern hemisphere. Deutsch calls this capacity reach:

这些都不是这个解释"出生"时要解决的问题。它生来解释地球的冬天，却顺手解释了火星、预测了南半球。Deutsch 把这种能力叫做 reach：

A good explanation's reach is infinite. A correct explanation doesn't only apply to the circumstances it was created for — in principle it applies to the whole universe. Demeter's grief explains nothing but winter, and even winter badly. The tilted axis: one explanation, explains wherever it goes.

好解释的 reach 是无限的。一个正确的解释不只适用于它出生的情境，原则上适用于整个宇宙。得墨忒耳的悲伤除了冬天什么都解释不了——连冬天都解释得很糟。而地轴倾斜，一个解释，走到哪里解释到哪里。

This is the first layer of "infinity" in the book's title: good explanations have no boundary.

这就是书名"无穷的开始"里"无穷"的第一层意思：好解释没有边界。

We do not test every testable theory, but only the few that we find are good explanations.
我们并不是检验每一个可检验的理论，而只检验那些我们认为是好解释的少数理论。

Testing is not the starting point of science. First comes a good explanation; only then is it worth testing.

检验不是科学的起点。先有好解释，才值得检验。

NIGHT 02 · 第二夜

CHAPTER 2 · CLOSER TO REALITY更接近实在

Tonight answers just one question: explanations are guesses — so how do guesses get closer and closer to being right? The answer starts with a student, a photographic plate, and a galaxy that wasn't there.

今夜只回答一个问题：解释都是猜的，猜怎么会越猜越对？答案从一个学生、一张底片、一个不存在的星系开始。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe student who counted galaxies数星系的学生

As a graduate student, Deutsch was once shown the Palomar Sky Survey — dramatic photographs of clusters of galaxies, fuzzy shapes against the black, set against the sharp points of nearby stars. The scale was overwhelming: whole galaxies, each holding billions of stars, reduced to smudges on a plate.

做研究生时，Deutsch 有一次看到了帕洛玛巡天（Palomar Sky Survey）的照片——成团的星系，模糊的光斑，对比附近恒星锐利的光点。尺度大得吓人：整个星系，几十亿颗星星，在底片上只剩一团模糊。

His job was to catalogue those galaxies — to look at photographic plates, by eye, and decide what was a galaxy and what was not. The tools were rudimentary. The work was slow. And it was, Deutsch says, creative and fun — not mindless toil.

他的工作是给这些星系编目——用肉眼看底片，判断什么是星系、什么不是。工具很原始，工作很慢。但 Deutsch 说，这工作有创造性，而且好玩——不是无脑苦力。

Already, a question is hiding in this scene: when you "just look" at a plate, are you seeing reality — or are you already interpreting it?

这个场景里已经藏了一个问题：当你"只是看看"底片时，你看到的是实在，还是已经在解读它了？

SCENE 2 · THE STORYThe galaxy that wasn't there不存在的星系

One day, a student mistook a flaw in the photograph for a galaxy. A smudge on the plate — a defect in the emulsion, a speck of dust — looked exactly like a distant galaxy. It was catalogued. For a while, it was a galaxy.

有一天，一个学生把底片上的一个瑕疵当成了星系。底片上的一团污迹——感光层的一个缺陷、一粒灰尘——看起来和一个遥远的星系一模一样。它被编进了目录。有一阵子，它就是一个星系。

Whose mistake was it? Not the eyes — the eyes saw the smudge correctly. The mistake was in the theory: the interpretation that said "this smudge is a galaxy." The error lived in the explanation, not in the observation.

这是谁的错？不是眼睛的错——眼睛正确地看到了那团污迹。错在理论里：那个"这团污迹是个星系"的解读。错误住在解释里，不住在观察里。

That is the first crack in a very old idea: that knowledge comes from looking. If looking were enough, a smudge could never become a galaxy. But it did — because looking is never just looking.

这就是一个古老想法的第一道裂缝：知识来自"看"。如果"看"就够了，一团污迹永远变不成星系。可它变成了——因为"看"从来不只是"看"。

The growth of knowledge consists of correcting misconceptions in our theories.
知识的增长，就是纠正我们理论中的误解。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNYou never see anything directly你从未直接看到任何东西

Here is Deutsch's blunt claim, the sentence this whole night turns on:

下面是 Deutsch 直白的论断，今夜整章都绕着这句话转：

We observe nothing directly anyway. All observation is theory-laden.
反正我们什么都不是直接观察到的。所有的观察都负载着理论。

Theory-laden: every observation already carries a theory inside it. Your eyes don't hand you the world raw. They hand you an interpretation — and your brain builds the rest.

Theory-laden（负载理论）：每一次观察里面都已经装着一个理论。你的眼睛递给你的不是原生的世界，而是一个解读——剩下的由你的大脑补完。

We never perceive anything directly. All our external experience is virtual reality... generated by our own brains.
我们从未直接感知任何东西。我们所有的外部经验都是虚拟现实……由我们自己的大脑生成。

Read that again: virtual reality. Not as a metaphor for headsets — as a description of ordinary seeing. What you call "seeing the world" is your brain running a simulation, built from signals, corrected by theories you already hold. The world out there is real. But the picture in your head is a construction.

再读一遍：虚拟现实。不是指头盔的比喻，而是对日常"看"的描述。你说的"看到世界"，是大脑在跑一个模拟——用信号搭建，用你已有的理论修正。外面的世界是真实的，但你脑子里的画面是建构出来的。

That is why appearances can be deceptive. And it is also why we — and our instruments — can correct for that deceptiveness. A deception you can correct is not a wall. It is a door.

所以表象会骗人。也正因为如此，我们——和我们的仪器——才能纠正这种骗人。能被纠正的欺骗不是一堵墙，而是一扇门。

That is why appearances can be deceptive, and it is also why we, and our instruments, can correct for that deceptiveness.
这就是为什么表象会骗人，也正是为什么我们——和我们的仪器——能够纠正这种骗人。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTThe telescope brings you closer望远镜让你更近

Now the obvious worry: if all observation is interpretation, and instruments add even more layers of it — telescopes, detectors, computers processing data — then surely instruments take us farther from reality, not closer. Astronomers don't even look at the sky any more. They stare at data.

现在一个明显的担心来了：如果所有观察都是解读，而仪器又加了更多层解读——望远镜、探测器、电脑处理数据——那仪器岂不是让我们离实在更远，而不是更近？天文学家现在根本不看天了，只盯着数据。

Deutsch says: exactly backwards. It may seem strange —

Deutsch 说：正好反了。这听起来很奇怪——

It may seem strange that scientific instruments bring us closer to reality when in purely physical terms they only ever separate us further from it.
科学仪器让我们更接近实在，这听起来可能很奇怪——因为从纯物理的角度说，它们只会让我们离实在更远。

But "closer" was never about physical distance. Your naked eye tells you a star is a white dot. A telescope tells you it is a massive nuclear furnace, millions of kilometres across. Which one is closer to reality? The telescope — because it corrects the error your eye makes.

但"更近"从来不是物理距离的问题。你的肉眼告诉你，星星是一个白点。望远镜告诉你，它是一个巨大的核熔炉，直径几百万公里。哪一个更接近实在？望远镜——因为它纠正了你的眼睛犯的错。

Telescopes... are rungs on a ladder of theoretical interpretation which reaches down to the true, unseen reality.
望远镜……是理论解读之梯上的一级，通向真实的、看不见的实在。

Think of it as a ladder. Each rung is a better theory. Your eye is one rung. The telescope is a higher rung — a theory built into glass and metal, tested and corrected. You climb the ladder not by getting physically nearer, but by getting the interpretation righter.

把它想成一架梯子。每一级都是一个更好的理论。你的眼睛是一级，望远镜是更高的一级——一个用玻璃和金属造出来的理论，经过检验和修正。你爬梯子靠的不是物理上更近，而是解读得更对。

So the astronomer who never looks at the sky is not farther from reality. She is closer — because she is using a better ladder.

所以那个从不看天的天文学家，离实在不是更远，而是更近——因为她用的梯子更好。

SCENE 5 · THE RISEKnowledge grows by correcting mistakes知识靠纠错生长

Put the pieces together and you get the engine of the whole book. Knowledge does not grow by collecting observations. It grows by correcting misconceptions.

把这些拼起来，就是整本书的引擎。知识不是靠收集观察来增长的，而是靠纠正误解来增长的。

The growth of knowledge consists of correcting misconceptions in our theories.
知识的增长，就是纠正我们理论中的误解。

Each correction moves the theory a little closer to reality. There is no ceiling to this: reality is comprehensible, and our error-correcting has no built-in stopping point. That is the second layer of "infinity" in the title — our explanations can approach reality without end.

每一次纠正，都让理论离实在近一点。这没有天花板：实在是可以被理解的，而我们的纠错没有内置的终点。这就是书名"无穷"的第二层意思——我们的解释可以无止境地接近实在。

And the work itself is not drudgery. Edison said research is "one per cent inspiration and ninety-nine per cent perspiration." Deutsch says that is misleading: people can apply creativity even to tasks that machines do uncreatively. Cataloguing galaxies by eye was repetitive — and it was creative, and fun. The toil can be creative, just as the discovery is.

而且这工作本身不是苦役。爱迪生说，研究是"百分之一的灵感加百分之九十九的汗水"。Deutsch 说，这话误导人：人即使在机器只会机械重复的任务里，也能发挥创造性。用肉眼给星系编目是重复的——同时它是有创造性的、好玩的。苦力也可以有创造性，和发现一样。

So: guesses get closer to being right not by piling up more observations, but by finding the misconceptions inside the guess and correcting them. That is how a smudge stops being a galaxy — and how a white dot becomes a furnace.

所以：猜越猜越对，靠的不是堆更多观察，而是找到猜里面的误解、纠正它。一团污迹就是这样不再是星系的，一个白点就是这样变成熔炉的。

NIGHT 03 · 第三夜

CHAPTER 3 · THE SPARK火花

Tonight answers just one question: what happened the first time humans "explained" the world? The answer starts with a winter that was someone's anger, and ends with a spark that could light up the universe.

今夜只回答一个问题：人类第一次"解释"世界时，发生了什么？答案从一个"有人发怒"的冬天开始，到一粒能点亮宇宙的火花结束。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe winter that was someone's anger发怒的冬天

Long before science, every explanation had a person in it. Winter came because someone was sad. The harvest was generous because someone was pleased. A disaster struck because someone was angry. The gods and spirits behind the weather were people, too — bigger, more powerful, but people all the same, with intentions and moods.

在科学出现之前，每一个解释里都有一个人。冬天来了，是因为有人伤心；收成好，是因为有人高兴；灾难降临，是因为有人发怒。天气背后的神和精灵也是人——更大、更强，但终究是人，有意图、有情绪。

And we were at the centre of it all — literally. The geocentric theory placed humans at the physical hub of the universe. Two kinds of anthropocentrism — explanatory and geometrical — made each other more plausible: the world was about us, and it was built around us.

而我们就在这一切的中心——字面意义上的中心。地心说把人类放在宇宙的物理中心。两种人类中心主义（anthropocentrism）——解释上的和几何上的——互相印证：世界是关于我们的，世界是围着我们建的。

There was one exception, and almost no one noticed it. The ancient Greek mathematician Euclid built geometry out of impersonal things — points and lines — and elegant axioms about them. It would later inspire the pioneers of the Enlightenment. But at the time, most astronomers were also astrologers: they used sophisticated geometry to read politics and personal fortunes in the stars.

有一个例外，几乎没人注意到。古希腊数学家欧几里得用非人的东西——点和线——加上优雅的公理，建起了几何学。它后来启发了启蒙运动的先驱。但在当时，大多数天文学家同时也是占星家：他们用精密的几何学，从星星里读政治、读个人命运。

So here is the question hiding in this scene: what if the world has no intentions at all? What if winter is not someone's sadness, and the stars do not care what we do?

这个场景里藏着一个问题：如果世界根本没有任何意图呢？如果冬天不是谁的伤心，星星也不在乎我们做什么呢？

SCENE 2 · THE STORYTwo modern myths两则现代神话

We abandoned the gods. But two new ideas took their place — and both sound scientific. The first is called the Principle of Mediocrity: there is nothing significant about humans, in the cosmic scheme of things. The physicist Stephen Hawking put it like this:

我们抛弃了神。但两个新想法取代了它们的位置——而且听起来都很科学。第一个叫平庸原理（Principle of Mediocrity）：在宇宙图景中，人类没什么重要性。物理学家霍金是这么说的：

Humans are 'just a chemical scum on the surface of a typical planet that's in orbit round a typical star on the outskirts of a typical galaxy'.
人类不过是"一颗典型行星表面的一层化学渣滓，绕着一颗典型恒星运行，而那颗恒星位于一个典型星系的边缘"。

The second idea is called Spaceship Earth. Imagine a generation ship — a spaceship on a journey so long that many generations live out their lives in transit. The biosphere, the system of all living things on Earth, is that ship: a vastly complex life-support system that provides everything we need. If we overload it, it breaks down — and, like the passengers, we have nowhere else to go.

第二个想法叫地球飞船（Spaceship Earth）。想象一艘世代飞船——航程如此漫长，好几代人在途中度过一生。生物圈，也就是地球上所有生物组成的系统，就是这艘飞船：一套极其复杂的生命维持系统，提供我们需要的一切。如果我们把它压垮了，它就会崩溃——而我们和船上的乘客一样，无处可去。

Both ideas agree on one thing: we live inside a tiny, friendly bubble in a hostile universe, and outside the bubble our reach ends. One bubble is physical — the biosphere. The other is conceptual — the limits of our brains. Both oppose arrogance: the Principle of Mediocrity opposes the arrogance of believing ourselves significant; Spaceship Earth opposes the arrogance of aspiring to control the world.

这两个想法在一个点上是一致的：我们住在一个充满敌意的宇宙里的小小友好气泡里，气泡之外，我们的 reach 就到头了。一个气泡是物理的——生物圈；另一个是概念的——我们大脑的极限。两者都反对傲慢：平庸原理反对"相信自己重要"的傲慢；地球飞船反对"妄想控制世界"的傲慢。

They sound humble. They sound scientific. Deutsch says they are both false — so false that their negations are worth carving in stone.

它们听起来很谦逊，听起来很科学。Deutsch 说，它们都是错的——错到它们的否定值得刻在石头上。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNThe negations, carved in stone刻在石头上的否定

Here are the two sentences Deutsch would carve in stone:

下面就是 Deutsch 要刻在石头上的两句话：

People are significant in the cosmic scheme of things; and the Earth's biosphere is incapable of supporting human life.
人在宇宙图景中是重要的；而地球生物圈没有能力维持人类生命。

Start with the biosphere. Deutsch is writing in Oxford, England, where winter nights are often cold enough to kill any human unprotected by clothing and other technology. Primeval Oxfordshire — Oxfordshire as it was before humans — might kill him in a matter of hours. The Great Rift Valley in eastern Africa, where our species evolved, was barely more hospitable: no safe water supply, no medical equipment, infested with predators, parasites and disease. "There are very few fossils of old people."

先说生物圈。Deutsch 在英格兰牛津写作，那里的冬夜冷到足以冻死任何没有衣服和其他技术保护的人。原始状态的牛津郡——人类出现之前的牛津郡——可能几小时内就要了他的命。东非大裂谷，我们物种演化的地方，也好不到哪去：没有安全的饮水，没有医疗设备，到处是捕食者、寄生虫和疾病。"老人的化石非常少。"

There is a life-support system in Oxfordshire today — but it was not provided by the biosphere. It has been built by humans. It consists of clothes, houses, farms, hospitals, an electrical grid, a sewage system and so on. Nearly the whole of the Earth's biosphere in its primeval state was likewise incapable of keeping an unprotected human alive for long. It would be much more accurate to call it a death trap for humans than a life-support system.

今天的牛津郡确实有一套生命维持系统——但它不是生物圈提供的，而是人类建造的。它由衣服、房子、农场、医院、电网、下水道等等组成。地球生物圈原始状态下的绝大部分，同样没有能力让一个没有保护的人活多久。说它是人类的死亡陷阱，远比说它是生命维持系统准确。

The biosphere no more provides humans with a life-support system than it provides us with radio telescopes.
生物圈为人类提供生命维持系统，不比它为我们提供射电望远镜多。

To the extent that we are on a "spaceship", Deutsch writes, we have never been merely its passengers, nor its stewards, nor even its maintenance crew: we are its designers and builders. Before the designs created by humans, it was not a vehicle, but only a heap of dangerous raw materials.

Deutsch 写道，如果说我们在一艘"飞船"上，那我们从来不只是它的乘客，不是它的管家，甚至不是它的维修工：我们是它的设计者和建造者。在人类创造的设计出现之前，它不是一艘飞船，只是一堆危险的原材料。

And the Principle of Mediocrity? It is itself anthropocentric: it singles out anthropocentrism for special blame, while judging us with human values like "arrogance" and "mediocrity". But it was never arrogance that kept people from better explanations. They did not know how to seek them. In a sense, Deutsch writes, their whole problem was that they were not arrogant enough: they assumed far too easily that the world was fundamentally incomprehensible to them.

那平庸原理呢？它本身就是人类中心主义的：它单把人类中心主义挑出来特别谴责，用的却是"傲慢""平庸"这些人类价值观。但让人们迟迟得不到更好解释的，从来不是傲慢。他们是不知道怎么去寻找更好的解释。某种意义上，Deutsch 写道，他们的问题恰恰是傲慢得不够：他们太轻易地认定，世界对他们来说从根本上是不可理解的。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTReach: wings vs. knowledgeReach：翅膀与知识

Why do human abilities have unlimited reach, when every other adaptation in the biosphere is bounded? Because of what the reach is made of. Imagine a flock of birds blown to a new island. Their wings and eyes still work — because wings and eyes exploit universal laws of physics, aerodynamics and optics. But fly those same birds a few kilometres straight up, and their wings stop working: the air is too thin. Every biological adaptation has limited reach — it works only inside the conditions it evolved for.

为什么人类的能力 reach 无限，而生物圈里其他每一种适应都是有限的？因为 reach 的构成不同。想象一群鸟被风吹到一座新岛上，它们的翅膀和眼睛照样管用——因为翅膀和眼睛利用的是物理学的普遍定律，空气动力学和光学。但把同样的鸟往上送几公里，翅膀就失效了：空气太稀薄。每一种生物适应都只有有限的 reach——只在它演化出来的条件里管用。

Human reach is different in kind. Consider all the conceivable transformations of physical objects. Some never happen because they are forbidden by the laws of nature. The rest are possible — but happen only when the requisite knowledge is present. And those are the only possibilities. That is the dichotomy this whole chapter turns on:

人类的 reach 在种类上就不同。想想物理对象所有可能的变化：有些永远不会发生，因为它们被自然定律禁止；其余的都是可能的——但只有在具备所需知识时才会发生。除此之外没有第三种可能。这就是整章绕着转的那个二分法：

Everything that is not forbidden by laws of nature is achievable, given the right knowledge.
凡是不被自然定律禁止的事，有了正确的知识都能做到。

That is why Deutsch defines a person as an entity that can create explanatory knowledge. It is also why human bodies are universal constructors: "factories for transforming anything into anything that the laws of nature allow." Take vitamin C. Every primate needs it; their genes cannot make it. Humans cause over a tonne of vitamin C to appear in Oxfordshire every week — from farms and factories. The same goes for air on the moon: oxygen is plentiful there in the form of metal oxides in moon rock. Even with present-day technology, a self-sufficient colony on the moon, powered by sunlight, is possible.

这就是为什么 Deutsch 把人（person）定义为"能创造解释性知识的实体"。也是为什么人体是通用构造器（universal constructor）："把任何东西变成任何东西的工厂，只要自然定律允许"。拿维生素 C 来说：所有灵长类都需要它，基因却造不出来。人类每周让一吨多的维生素 C 出现在牛津郡——来自农场和工厂。月球上的空气也一样：月岩里的金属氧化物中氧很丰富。即使以今天的技术，一个靠阳光供电、自给自足的月球殖民地也是可能的。

And the "perspiration" — the hard, repetitive work? Remember Edison from last night: "one per cent inspiration and ninety-nine per cent perspiration." Deutsch says the perspiration phase can always be automated — just as the task of recognizing galaxies on photographic plates was. The more advanced technology becomes, the shorter the gap between inspiration and automation.

那"汗水"呢——那些艰苦重复的工作？还记得上夜的爱迪生吗："百分之一的灵感加百分之九十九的汗水"。Deutsch 说，"汗水"阶段永远可以自动化——就像在底片上识别星系的任务一样。技术越先进，灵感与自动化之间的间隔就越短。

So the difference between us and every other species is not that we are smarter. It is what kind of knowledge we can use — explanatory instead of rule-of-thumb — and how we create it: conjecture and criticism of ideas, rather than the variation and selection of genes.

所以我们和其他物种的区别不在于我们更聪明，而在于我们能用什么样的知识——解释性的，而不是经验法则；以及我们怎么创造它——靠猜想和批评，而不是基因的变异与选择。

And here is the strangest proof that people are significant. In Arecibo, Puerto Rico, there is a giant radio telescope listening for signals from extraterrestrial intelligence. In an office near the telescope there is a small refrigerator — and inside it, a bottle of champagne, sealed by a cork. That cork will pop the day the search succeeds. To predict whether it pops, you must know whether there are people sending radio signals from other solar systems. The behaviour of that humble cork cannot be explained without understanding everything of fundamental importance.

而人很重要的最奇怪的证据是这个：在波多黎各的阿雷西博，有一台巨大的射电望远镜，正在监听外星智慧的信号。望远镜附近一间办公室里有一台小冰箱——里面放着一瓶香槟，瓶口塞着软木塞。搜索成功的那一天，塞子就会弹开。要预测它会不会弹开，你必须知道其他太阳系里有没有人在发无线电信号。这枚小小软木塞的行为，不理解一切根本重要的东西就解释不了。

SCENE 5 · THE RISETwo tablets, one spark两块石碑，一个火花

Two more truths deserve to be carved in stone. The first: problems are inevitable. "It is inevitable that we face problems, but no particular problem is inevitable." The second: problems are soluble. "By 'soluble' I mean that the right knowledge would solve them."

还有两个真理值得刻在石头上。第一：问题不可避免（problems are inevitable）。"我们必然会面对问题，但没有哪一个具体问题是必然的。"第二：问题可以解决（problems are soluble）。"所谓'可以解决'，我的意思是正确的知识能解决它们。"

Make it concrete. During any given century there is about one chance in a thousand that the Earth will be struck by a comet or asteroid large enough to kill at least a substantial proportion of all human beings. Deutsch points out that a typical child born in the United States today is more likely to die as a result of an astronomical event than a car accident. We have no defence against that yet — unless we create the knowledge. As Hawking said: "I don't think the human race will survive the next thousand years, unless we spread into space... I'm an optimist. We will reach out to the stars."

说具体点。在任何一个世纪里，地球都有大约千分之一的概率被一颗大到足以杀死相当一部分人类的彗星或小行星撞上。Deutsch 指出，今天出生在美国的一个普通孩子，死于天文事件的概率比死于车祸还高。我们现在对此毫无防御——除非我们创造出相应的知识。霍金说过："我认为人类活不过接下来的一千年，除非我们走向太空……我是乐观主义者。我们会走向群星。"

And here is the deepest point in the chapter. An unproblematic life would not be a good life:

而这一章最深的一点是：没有问题的生活不是好生活：

An unproblematic state is a state without creative thought. Its other name is death.
没有问题的状态，就是没有创造性思想的状态。它的另一个名字是死亡。

Nor will we ever run out of problems. "The deeper an explanation is, the more new problems it creates." There is no such thing as an ultimate explanation — just as "the gods did it" is always a bad explanation, any foundation that explains everything must be bad too, because it cannot answer: why that foundation and not another?

而且我们永远不会用完问题。"解释越深，创造的新问题就越多。"不存在终极解释——正如"神干的"永远是坏解释，任何号称能解释一切的基础也一定是坏的，因为它回答不了：为什么是这个基础，而不是另一个？

Now, the spark itself. Take the most typical place in the universe: a cube of intergalactic space, the size of our solar system — cold, dark, empty, less than one atom per cubic metre. Even that cube holds over a billion tonnes of matter. And it is full of evidence: on any clear night, the chances are that your roof will be struck by evidence falling from the sky which, if you only knew what to look for and how, would win you a Nobel prize. Matter, energy, evidence — the only three things an environment needs in order to host the open-ended creation of knowledge. And nearly every environment has them.

现在说火花本身。拿宇宙中最典型的地方来说：一块星际空间的立方体，和我们的太阳系一样大——冷、黑、空，每立方米不到一个原子。就连这块立方体里也有十亿吨以上的物质。而且它满是证据：在任何一个晴朗的夜晚，你家屋顶都可能被从天上掉下来的证据击中——如果你知道要找什么、怎么找，它能让你拿诺贝尔奖。物质、能量、证据——一个环境要承载无止境的知识创造，只需要这三样。而几乎每个环境都有它们。

Like an explosive awaiting a spark, unimaginably numerous environments in the universe are waiting out there, for aeons on end, doing nothing at all. Almost any of them would, if the right knowledge ever reached it, instantly and irrevocably burst into intense knowledge-creation. And when knowledge lands — on the moon, say — the moon reorganizes itself, and starts to create an indefinitely long stream of ever-improving explanations. A beginning of infinity.

就像等待火花的炸药，宇宙中多得难以想象的环境在外面等着，等了亿万年，什么也不做。几乎任何一个，只要正确的知识一旦到达，就会立刻、不可逆转地爆发出高强度的知识创造。而当知识落地——比如落在月球上——月球会重新组织自己，开始创造一条无限长的、不断改进的解释之流。一个无穷的开始。

If we want to, we could be that spark.
如果我们愿意，我们可以成为那粒火花。

We shall always be at the beginning of infinity. That is not a failure. That is the spark.

我们将永远处在无穷的开始。这不是失败，这就是火花。

NIGHT 04 · 第四夜

CHAPTER 4 · CREATION创造

Tonight answers just one question: with no creator, how does the universe make new things? The answer starts with a watch on a heath, and ends with you — a universal constructor.

今夜只回答一个问题：没有创造者，宇宙怎么造出新东西？答案从荒野上的一块表开始，到你结束——一个通用构造器。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe watch on the heath荒野上的一块表

In 1802, the English theologian William Paley asked you to imagine a walk across a heath. You trip over a stone. You would not think twice: stones have always been there. But suppose you find a watch on the ground instead. You open it. Inside are gears and springs, each part fitted to the next, all working together to tell the time. Could the watch have been there for ever, with no maker? No — its design points to a designer.

1802 年，英国神学家 William Paley 请你想象一次荒野散步。你踢到一块石头，你不会多想：石头一直都在。但假如你在地上捡到一块表呢？你打开它，里面是齿轮和弹簧，每个零件严丝合缝，一起走时。这块表可能一直就在那儿、没有制造者吗？不可能——它的设计指向一个设计者。

Then Paley made his move: living things are like the watch, only vastly more intricate. An eye is a lens, an iris, a retina — parts fitted together to see. A wing is fitted to fly. If a watch needs a watchmaker, then eyes and wings need a maker too — and only a divine designer could be that maker. This is the argument from design, and for two centuries it was the strongest reason most people had for believing in a creator.

然后 Paley 出了关键一招：生物就像这块表，只不过复杂得多。眼睛是晶状体、虹膜、视网膜——零件严丝合缝，为看而生。翅膀为飞而生。如果表需要制表人，眼睛和翅膀也需要制造者——而只有神才能做这个制造者。这就是设计论证（argument from design），两百年来，它是大多数人相信创造者最有力的理由。

The argument feels airtight. It has one problem: it explains nothing. A designer who can design an eye must be at least as complex as the eye itself — so who designed the designer? The argument does not remove the mystery; it moves it one step back and declares it solved.

这个论证听起来天衣无缝，只有一个问题：它什么都没解释。一个能设计眼睛的设计者，本身至少得和眼睛一样复杂——那谁设计了设计者？这个论证没有消除谜团，只是把它往后挪了一步，就宣布解决了。

And there is a second problem, one Paley could not have imagined: the watch can be made with no watchmaker at all.

还有第二个问题，是 Paley 做梦也想不到的：表也可以在没有制表人的情况下造出来。

SCENE 2 · THE STORYThe appearance of design看起来像设计

Look at an eye. It looks designed — because it is, in a sense. The lens focuses light, the iris adjusts, the retina catches the image. But Darwin found a way to get design without a designer. It needs only three ingredients: variation, selection, and time.

看一只眼睛，它看起来像被设计过——某种意义上说，它确实是。晶状体聚焦光线，虹膜调节进光，视网膜捕捉图像。但 Darwin 找到了一条没有设计者也能得到设计的路。只需要三种原料：变异、选择、时间。

Start with a population of creatures that differ slightly from one another — some see a little better, some a little worse. The better seers survive more often and have more offspring. Their children inherit the better eyes, vary a little more, and the cycle repeats. Each step is tiny, and each step is blind — no one is aiming at an eye. But after millions of generations, the small steps add up to something that looks exactly like design. The knowledge of how to see is stored in the genes.

从一群彼此略有不同的生物开始——有的看得清楚一点，有的差一点。看得清楚的活得更久、后代更多。它们的孩子继承更好的眼睛，再变异一点，循环往复。每一步都很小，每一步都是盲目的——没有人在瞄准一只眼睛。但经过几百万代，小步子累积成了看起来完全像设计的东西。关于"怎么看"的知识，存在基因里。

There is grandeur in this view of life... from so simple a beginning endless forms most beautiful and most wonderful have been, and are being, evolved. — Charles Darwin, On the Origin of Species (1859)
"生命的这种观点自有其壮丽之处……从如此简单的开端，演化出了无数最美丽、最奇妙的形态，而且仍在演化。"——Charles Darwin，《物种起源》（1859）

That is the trick, and it is the most important trick in the chapter: variation and selection can create knowledge — knowledge of how to see, how to fly, how to survive — without anyone knowing anything. Evolution is a creator that does not need a mind. And once you see the trick, you see it everywhere: the biosphere built itself the same way, one small kept improvement at a time.

这就是关键一招，也是这一章最重要的一招：变异加选择可以创造知识——关于怎么看、怎么飞、怎么活的知识——而不需要任何人知道任何事。演化是一位不需要心智的创造者。一旦你看懂这一招，你会发现它无处不在：生物圈就是这样一小步一小步自己建起来的。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNA fine-tuned universe微调的宇宙

But wait. The design argument has a modern cousin, and it is harder to dismiss. The laws of physics themselves look tuned for life. Change the strength of gravity a little, or the mass of an electron, and stars never form, atoms never hold together, life never gets started. The universe looks as if its dials were set just right — for us.

但等等。设计论证还有一个现代亲戚，更难打发。物理定律本身看起来就是为生命微调过的。把引力强度改一点点，或把电子质量改一点点，恒星就不会形成，原子就无法结合，生命永远不会开始。宇宙看起来像是有人把刻度盘调得刚刚好——为我们。

Three explanations are on the table. One: luck — the dials just happened to land right, and we are here to notice it. Two: design — someone set the dials, which brings back the old question: who set the setter? Three: the multiverse — there are countless universes with different dials, and we find ourselves in one of the rare ones where life is possible, the way a fish finds itself in water.

桌上有三种解释。一：运气——刻度盘碰巧落在了正确的位置，而我们恰好在这里注意到它。二：设计——有人调了刻度盘，但老问题又回来了：谁调了调盘子的人？三：多重宇宙——有无数个宇宙，刻度盘各不相同，而我们发现自己身处其中一个生命成为可能的罕见宇宙，就像鱼发现自己在水里。

Deutsch does not settle this in this chapter — the multiverse gets its own night later. What he settles here is the method: a good explanation must not explain the mystery by assuming a bigger mystery. "Someone designed it" is not an explanation; it is a postponement. And "it was luck" is not an explanation either — it is a refusal to explain. The fine-tuning of the universe is a real problem, and it is still open. But the shape of an answer is already visible: whatever made the dials, it was a process, not a person.

Deutsch 在这一章里没有把这个问题定下来——多重宇宙自有它的一夜。他在这里定下来的是方法：一个好解释不能用一个更大的谜来解释谜。"有人设计了它"不是解释，是拖延；"这就是运气"也不是解释，是拒绝解释。宇宙的微调（fine-tuning）是一个真正的问题，至今仍未解决。但答案的形状已经可见：无论是什么造了这些刻度盘，它是一个过程，不是一个人。

And there is a clue hiding in the word "tuned". A tune implies a tuner only if you assume the dials were set from outside. But the dials are the laws themselves — and the laws do not need to be set. They are what they are, and everything else — stars, planets, life — is what the laws make possible.

而"微调"这个词里藏着一个线索。"调过"意味着"有人调"，前提是你假定刻度盘是从外面被设定好的。但刻度盘本身就是定律——定律不需要被设定。它们就是它们，而其他一切——恒星、行星、生命——都是定律所允许的东西。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTTwo kinds of creation两种创造

Now the chapter draws its sharpest line. There are two kinds of creation in the universe, and they could not be more different. The first is blind creation: variation and selection, over deep time. It made the eye, the wing, the biosphere. It is slow, wasteful, and it never knows what it is doing — but it works, because the laws of physics allow it to.

现在这一章画出了最锋利的一条线。宇宙里有两种创造，截然不同。第一种是盲目的创造：变异加选择，经过漫长的时间。它造出了眼睛、翅膀、生物圈。它缓慢、浪费、永远不知道自己在做什么——但它管用，因为物理定律允许它管用。

The second is the creation of knowledge: conjecture and criticism. A human guesses an explanation, tests it, finds the error, guesses better. Unlike evolution, this creation knows what it is doing — or at least, it can find out. And unlike evolution, it accelerates: each new piece of knowledge makes the next one easier to create. Genes took billions of years to build an eye; human knowledge built a telescope in a few centuries, and a radio telescope in a few decades.

第二种是知识的创造：猜想与批评。人类猜一个解释，检验它，找到错误，猜得更好。和演化不同，这种创造知道自己在做什么——至少，它能发现自己在做什么。和演化不同，它还会加速：每一份新知识都让下一份更容易创造。基因花了几十亿年造出一只眼睛；人类知识几个世纪造出了望远镜，几十年造出了射电望远镜。

Here is the difference that matters: blind creation stores its knowledge in genes, and genes can only be copied. Knowledge creation stores its knowledge in explanations — and explanations can be shared, criticized, combined, and improved by anyone. That is why the second kind of creation has no speed limit. That is why the beginning of infinity is the beginning of knowledge, not the beginning of life.

关键的区别在这里：盲目的创造把知识存在基因里，基因只能被复制。知识的创造把知识存在解释里——而解释可以被任何人分享、批评、组合、改进。所以第二种创造没有速度上限。所以无穷的开始，是知识的开始，不是生命的开始。

SCENE 5 · THE PAYOFFThe first constructor第一个构造器

So who built the first constructor? No one. That is the whole point. Evolution built us — no designer, no plan, no intention — and we turned out to be universal constructors: beings who can turn anything into anything the laws of physics allow. Clothes from cotton, medicine from mould, computers from sand. The biosphere built itself the same way, and so did the stars before it.

那么，第一个构造器是谁造的？没有人。这就是全部要点。演化造出了我们——没有设计者、没有计划、没有意图——而我们成了通用构造器（universal constructors）：能把任何东西变成任何东西，只要物理定律允许。棉花变衣服，霉菌变药物，沙子变电脑。生物圈也是这样自己建起来的，在它之前的恒星也是。

And that means creation is not something that happened once, long ago, and stopped. It is happening now, and it has no end in sight — because the laws of physics do not forbid new things, they permit them. The universe has been creating since the beginning. What is new is that, with knowledge, creation has a creator that knows what it is doing: us.

这意味着创造不是很久以前发生过一次就停了的事。它正在发生，而且看不到尽头——因为物理定律不是禁止新事物，而是允许它们。宇宙从一开始就在创造。新的东西是：有了知识，创造有了一个知道自己在做什么的创造者：我们。

Next time someone tells you something must have been designed, ask them the question this night taught you: who designed the designer? And then ask the better question: what process — variation, selection, conjecture, criticism — could have built it with no designer at all?

下次有人告诉你某样东西一定是被设计出来的，问他今夜教你的问题：谁设计了设计者？然后问一个更好的问题：什么样的过程——变异、选择、猜想、批评——可以在没有设计者的情况下把它造出来？

NIGHT 05 · 第五夜

CHAPTER 5 · THE REALITY OF ABSTRACTIONS抽象实在

Tonight answers just one question: are numbers, laws, and explanations real things? The answer starts with cicadas that count in primes, and ends with you — a creature that makes abstractions.

今夜只回答一个问题：数字、定律、解释，是真实存在的东西吗？答案从用质数计数的蝉开始，到你结束——一个能造出抽象的生物。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe cicadas' secret蝉的秘密

Every thirteen or seventeen years, in the forests of eastern North America, the ground opens and millions of cicadas rise at once. They sing for a few weeks, lay their eggs, and die. Then silence — for thirteen years. Or seventeen. Never twelve. Never sixteen. Never fifteen.

每隔十三年或十七年，北美东部森林的地面裂开，数百万只蝉同时升起。它们唱几周，产下卵，死去。然后寂静——十三年。或十七年。从来不是十二年。从来不是十六年。从来不是十五年。

Before the argument starts, two facts to keep straight. First, these are periodical cicadas, genus Magicicada — they live only in the eastern half of the United States, about thirty states from New York to Georgia, and nowhere else on Earth. The cicadas you hear every summer in California are annual cicadas — Okanagana, Platypedia — a different group: they spend two to five years underground, but their generations overlap, so adults appear every year. They do not use the prime trick at all. Second, the periodical cicadas are divided into fifteen broods — twelve seventeen-year broods and three thirteen-year ones — each on its own clock, so somewhere in the east some brood emerges almost every year. In 2024 a thirteen-year brood and a seventeen-year brood emerged together, something that happens only once every 221 years — the first time since 1803. But one brood, in one forest, is silent for thirteen or seventeen years. That is the group the prime numbers protect.

论证开始之前，先说清两点。第一，这是周期蝉，Magicicada 属——只生活在美国东部一半的地方，大约三十个州，从纽约到佐治亚，地球上别处没有。你在加州每年夏天听到的蝉是一年蝉——Okanagana、Platypedia——是另一群：它们在地下待两到五年，但各代重叠，所以每年都有成虫出来。它们根本不用质数这招。第二，周期蝉分成十五群——十二群十七年蝉、三群十三年蝉——每群各有时钟，所以美国东部每年总有某地的一群出来。2024 年，一群十三年蝉和一群十七年蝉同时出来了，这种事 221 年才有一次，上一次是 1803 年。但同一群、在同一片林子，要沉默十三年或十七年。质数保护的，就是这一群。

Why those numbers? Thirteen and seventeen are both prime numbers — divisible only by one and themselves. A predator on a two-, three-, or four-year cycle would meet a twelve-year cicada every twelve years — at every single emergence. But the same predator meets a thirteen-year cicada only once every twenty-six, thirty-nine, or fifty-two years. Primes are the hardest cycles to synchronize with. The cicadas, blindly, over millions of years, found the numbers that keep them safest.

为什么是这两个数字？十三和十七都是质数（prime numbers）——只能被一和自己整除。一只两、三、四年周期的捕食者，每十二年就会撞上一次十二年蝉——每次出来都被撞上。但同样的捕食者，要二十六、三十九或五十二年才撞上一次十三年蝉。质数是最难同步的周期。蝉，盲目地，经过几百万年，找到了让它们最安全的数字。

Stop and notice what just happened. A fact about numbers — that thirteen is prime — changed what happens in a forest. No one taught the cicadas arithmetic. No cicada knows what a prime is. And yet the primality of thirteen decided who lived and who was eaten. The abstract did something physical.

停下来，注意刚才发生了什么。一个关于数字的事实——十三是质数——改变了森林里发生的事。没有人教蝉算术，没有一只蝉知道什么是质数。然而十三的质数性决定了谁活下来、谁被吃掉。抽象的东西做了物理的事。

That is tonight's question: are numbers real? Not "useful". Not "a convenient way to talk". Real — the way a stone is real, the way a cicada is real.

这就是今夜的问题：数字是真实的吗？不是"有用"，不是"一种方便的说法"。真实——像石头真实、像蝉真实那样真实。

SCENE 2 · THE STORYWhat "real" means"真实"是什么意思

Most people carry a simple test for reality: if you can touch it, it is real. A stone is real. A table is real. A number is not — you cannot pick up a seven and put it in your pocket. By that test, numbers are fictions: useful ones, invented by minds.

大多数人心里有一把简单的尺子：能摸到的，就是真实的。石头是真实的，桌子是真实的。数字不是——你捡不起一个七，放不进口袋。按这把尺子，数字是虚构，是有用的虚构，是头脑发明的。

Deutsch says the test is wrong — and the cicadas prove it. His test is different: something is real if it is indispensable to our best explanations of the world. A stone figures in explanations — why the window broke. A gene figures in explanations — why the eye sees. And thirteen figures in the explanation of why the cicadas come when they do. Take thirteen out of the explanation, and the explanation collapses. That is what "real" means.

Deutsch 说这把尺子是错的——蝉就是证据。他的标准不同：如果一个东西在我们对世界的最好解释里不可或缺，它就是真实的。石头出现在解释里——为什么窗户碎了。基因出现在解释里——为什么眼睛能看见。十三也出现在解释里——为什么蝉在那个时候出现。把十三从解释里拿走，解释就塌了。这就是"真实"的意思。

Chess makes the point even sharper. A game of chess is not a physical thing. You cannot weigh a checkmate. The rules are not written in the wood of the pieces — the same game can be played with plastic, with a computer, or entirely in your head. And yet the rules decide everything: which moves are legal, who wins, who loses. Money changes hands because of them. A rule you cannot touch moves real money. If that is not real, what is?

象棋把这个道理说得更锋利。一盘象棋不是一个物理的东西，你称不了一次"将死"的重量。规则不在棋子的木头里——同一盘棋可以用塑料下，可以用电脑下，可以完全在脑子里下。然而规则决定一切：哪些走法合法，谁赢，谁输。钱因为它们易手。一条你摸不着的规则挪动了真金白银。如果这不算真实，什么才算？

The laws of physics are the biggest example of all. You cannot point at a law of gravity. There is no physical object called "the law". And yet the laws constrain everything that happens — they are what make stars burn and eyes see. Night four already said it: the dials are the laws themselves, and the laws do not need to be set. They are real, and they are abstract.

物理定律是其中最大的例子。你指不了一条引力定律，没有一个叫"定律"的物理对象。然而定律约束着一切发生的事——它们让恒星燃烧、让眼睛看见。第四夜已经说过：刻度盘本身就是定律，定律不需要被设定。它们是真实的，而且是抽象的。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNTwo ways to get it wrong两种错法

There are two classic ways to get abstractions wrong, and both feel like common sense.

对抽象有两种经典的错法，两种听起来都像常识。

The first: only physical things are real. Numbers, laws, rules — just useful fictions, shorthand in our heads. The problem: fictions do not change forests. If thirteen were only a fiction, the cicadas would not care which years they emerge. If the laws of physics were only shorthand, they could not constrain a star. A fiction cannot do physical work. The moment something does work in the world, the "just a fiction" story fails.

第一种：只有物理的东西是真实的。数字、定律、规则——只是有用的虚构，只是头脑里的简写。问题是：虚构改变不了森林。如果十三只是虚构，蝉不会在乎哪年出来。如果物理定律只是简写，它们约束不了一颗恒星。虚构做不了物理的活。只要一样东西在世界里干了活，"只是虚构"的说法就站不住了。

The second: abstractions are real, but they live somewhere else — in a separate, perfect realm, the way Plato imagined. Numbers live in the heaven of forms; the physical world is just their shadow. The problem: if numbers live in another realm, how do they reach into this one? How does a perfect, untouchable thirteen tell a real cicada when to emerge? A separate realm explains nothing — it just moves the mystery somewhere you cannot check.

第二种：抽象是真实的，但它们住在别处——一个分开的、完美的领域，就像 Plato 想象的那样。数字住在理念的天堂里，物理世界只是它们的影子。问题是：如果数字住在另一个领域，它们怎么伸手够到这一个？一个完美的、摸不着的十三，怎么告诉一只真实的蝉什么时候出来？一个分开的领域什么都没解释——只是把谜挪到了你检查不到的地方。

Deutsch's answer is the middle path, and it is the only one that keeps the cicadas: abstractions are real here, in this world, because they figure in true explanations of this world. They do not need a separate heaven, and they are not fictions. They are part of the furniture of reality — the invisible part, the part that explains why the visible part behaves the way it does.

Deutsch 的答案是中间那条路，也是唯一留得住蝉的路：抽象在这里、在这个世界里是真实的，因为它们出现在关于这个世界的真解释里。它们不需要一个分开的天堂，也不是虚构。它们是实在家具的一部分——看不见的那部分，解释看得见的那部分为什么是这个样子的那部分。

And that gives us a test for telling real abstractions from mere fictions. A unicorn is an abstraction too — you can imagine it, describe it, draw it. But it figures in no true explanation of anything. Nothing in the world behaves differently because of unicorns. The test is not "can I imagine it?" — it is "does the best explanation of the world need it?" Numbers pass. Laws pass. Unicorns do not.

这也给了我们一把尺子，分辨真实的抽象和纯粹的虚构。独角兽也是一个抽象（abstraction）——你可以想象它、描述它、画它。但它没有出现在任何关于任何事的真解释里。世界里没有任何东西因为独角兽而表现不同。尺子不是"你能想象它吗？"，而是"关于世界的最好解释需要它吗？"数字通过了，定律通过了，独角兽没有。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTKnowledge is the ultimate abstraction知识是终极抽象

Now the chapter turns the lens on its own subject — knowledge. Knowledge is an abstraction too. An explanation is not a physical thing. You cannot weigh the explanation of why the sky is blue. And yet, once an explanation is created, it does the most physical work of anything in the universe: it moves matter. It builds telescopes, cures diseases, sends probes to other planets.

现在这一章把镜头转向了它自己的主题——知识。知识也是一个抽象。解释不是一个物理的东西，你称不了"天空为什么是蓝的"这个解释的重量。然而，解释一旦被创造出来，就做着宇宙里最物理的活：它挪动物质，造望远镜、治疾病、把探测器送到别的行星。

Here is the contrast that matters. A gene stores knowledge in one physical form only — the DNA molecule, copied from parent to child. An explanation stores knowledge in a form that can live anywhere: in a brain, in a book, in a computer, in a conversation. The content is the same; the storage changes. That is why the second kind of creation from last night has no speed limit: abstractions are not tied to one physical body. They can be copied, combined, criticized, and improved by anyone, anywhere.

关键的对照在这里。基因把知识存在唯一一种物理形式里——DNA 分子，从父母复制给孩子。解释把知识存在一种可以住在任何地方的形式里：大脑、书、电脑、对话。内容一样，载体在变。这就是为什么昨夜的第二种创造没有速度上限：抽象不被绑在某一个物理身体上。它们可以被任何人、在任何地方复制、组合、批评、改进。

Think of it this way: the same chess game can be played on wood, on a screen, or in your head — the game is the abstraction, and it does not care which physical stuff carries it. Knowledge is the same. The explanation of evolution does not care whether it lives in Darwin's notebook or in your mind tonight. What matters is the content — and content is abstract. That is why knowledge, once created, tends to stay created: you cannot un-create an abstraction by destroying one copy of it. Burn the book, and the explanation still lives in a thousand heads.

这么想：同一盘棋可以在木头上下、在屏幕上下、在脑子里下——棋是抽象，它不在乎哪种物理东西承载它。知识也一样。演化的解释不在乎它住在 Darwin 的笔记本里，还是今夜你的脑子里。重要的是内容——而内容是抽象的。所以知识一旦被创造出来，就倾向于留存下来：你毁不掉一个抽象，哪怕烧掉它的一份拷贝。烧掉书，解释还活在一千个脑子里。

And that is the deep link back to last night. Evolution creates knowledge slowly, stored in genes. Humans create knowledge fast, stored in explanations. Both are real — but only the second can spread without waiting for a generation to be born. The beginning of infinity is the beginning of knowledge, and knowledge is the most powerful abstraction there is.

这就是和昨夜的深层连接。演化缓慢地创造知识，存在基因里。人类快速地创造知识，存在解释里。两种都是真实的——但只有第二种能不等待一代人出生就传播开来。无穷的开始是知识的开始，而知识是最强大的抽象。

SCENE 5 · THE PAYOFFSo what is real?那么，什么是真实的？

So let us answer tonight's question plainly. Are numbers real? Yes — thirteen decides when cicadas emerge. Are laws real? Yes — they constrain every star and every eye. Are explanations real? And here is the payoff, the one that changes how you act: if you want to change the world, you do not only move matter — you create abstractions. A new explanation is a new real thing in the world, and once it exists, it starts moving matter on its own. NIGHT 06 · 第六夜

CHAPTER 6 · THE JUMP TO UNIVERSALITY普遍性一跃

Tonight answers just one question: how does something finite suddenly cover everything? The answer starts with a trick for spelling "treason", and ends with you — a universal explainer.

今夜只回答一个问题：有限的东西怎么突然覆盖了一切？答案从一个拼 "treason" 的小把戏开始，到你结束——一个普遍解释者。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe treason trick拼出 treason 的把戏

The earliest writing systems used stylised pictures — pictograms — to represent words or concepts. A picture of the sun meant "sun"; a picture of a tree meant "tree". Every new word needed a new picture. The system could grow forever and still never cover everything: there would always be a word with no picture yet.

最早的书写系统用风格化的图画——象形字——代表词或概念。一幅太阳的画表示"太阳"，一幅树的画表示"树"。每个新词都需要一幅新画。这个系统可以永远长下去，却永远覆盖不了全部：总会有一个词还没有画。

Then someone changed one small rule. Instead of building words out of other words, build them out of the initial sounds of other words. If English were written in pictograms, you could spell "treason" with the pictograms for Tent, Rock, EAgle, Zebra, Nose — T-R-EA-Z-N. That tiny change makes the system universal: it can now spell every word in the language, including words that have not been invented yet. It is thought that the earliest alphabets evolved from rules like that.

然后有人改了一个小规则。不用别的词来拼词，改用别的词的开头音来拼。如果英文用象形字写，你可以用 Tent（帐篷）、Rock（石头）、EAgle（鹰）、Zebra（斑马）、Nose（鼻子）的象形字拼出 "treason"——T-R-EA-Z-N。这一个小改动让系统变成了普遍的：它现在能拼出这门语言里的每一个词，包括还没被发明出来的词。人们认为最早的字母表就是从这样的规则演化来的。

Notice what did not happen. Nobody added ten thousand new pictograms. Nobody made the pictures better. One rule changed — and a system that could never finish suddenly could do everything. That is the jump to universality: the tendency of gradually improving systems to undergo a sudden large increase in functionality, becoming universal in some domain.

注意什么没有发生。没有人加了一万幅新象形字，没有人把画画得更好。只改了一条规则——一个永远完不成的系统突然什么都能做了。这就是普遍性一跃：逐步改进的系统突然大幅增加功能、在某个领域变成普遍的倾向。

SCENE 2 · THE STORYFrom tallying to counting从划杠到计数

Numerals are older than writing. They began as tally marks — one mark for each goat released from a pen, one crossed out for each goat that returned. Tallying is universal in its own small way: it always works. But tallying only says "another, and another, and another". Counting says "forty, forty-one, forty-two" — it treats numbers as things.

数字比文字更古老。它从划杠开始——放出一只羊划一道，回来一只划掉一道。划杠在自己的小天地里是普遍的：它永远管用。但划杠只会说"又一个，又一个，又一个"。计数会说"四十、四十一、四十二"——它把数字当作东西来对待。

The Romans built rules on top of tallying: place symbols side by side and add them; write them in decreasing order; replace adjacent symbols with the symbol for their combined value. With those rules, the system itself did the arithmetic — the human was only executing the program, the way a computer executes code. Prices, wages, taxes, interest: all became computable. But the Roman system was still bounded. It had a highest symbol, and beyond that you needed new symbols or new rules. Archimedes' Greek system, much like the Roman one, topped out at M — one myriad, 10,000. It could never be universal.

罗马人在划杠上加了规则：符号并排放就是相加；按从大到小排列；相邻符号能合并就换成合值符号。有了这些规则，系统自己会算术——人只是在执行程序，就像计算机执行代码。价格、工资、税、利息：全都能算了。但罗马系统仍然有界。它有个最大的符号，再往上就需要新符号或新规则。阿基米德的希腊系统和罗马系统很像，到 M 就到顶了——一万，10,000。它永远成不了普遍的。

The Babylonians, around 1900 BCE, came close: a place-value system with 59 digits. But the system we use today, which originated in India, needs only ten symbols — the digits 0 to 9 — and one rule: the value of a digit depends on its position. The digit 2 means two when written by itself, but two hundred in the numeral 204. The 0 is a placeholder: it holds the empty place so the 2 knows it means hundreds. Ten symbols plus one rule — and every number that exists, or could ever exist, already has a place in it.

巴比伦人大约在公元前 1900 年差点就做到了：一个有 59 个数字的位值系统。但我们今天用的系统起源于印度，只需要十个符号——数字 0 到 9——加一条规则：一个数字的值取决于它所在的位置。数字 2 单独写是二，在 204 里是二百。0 是占位符：它占住空位，好让 2 知道自己是百位。十个符号加一条规则——每一个存在或可能存在的数字，都已经在里面有了位置。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNThe machine jump机器一跃

Charles Babbage built the Difference Engine to do one kind of calculation: printing tables of numbers, the kind sailors and bankers needed. It was brilliant, and it was parochial — one job, done well. Then he designed the Analytical Engine. It would have been a universal computer — as he and Ada Lovelace understood. The jump was the same shape as the alphabet's: not a bigger machine, but a machine that could run any program.

查尔斯·巴贝奇造了差分机，只做一种计算：打印水手和银行家需要的数字表。它很出色，也很狭隘——一件事，做得很好。然后他设计了解析机。它会是一台通用计算机——他和阿达·洛芙莱斯都明白这一点。一跃的形状和字母表一样：不是更大的机器，而是能跑任何程序的机器。

Lovelace saw further than Babbage. She wrote that the Engine "weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard loom weaves flowers and leaves" — it did not have to manipulate numbers at all. Anything that could be reduced to rules — music, images, words — could in principle pass through it. A special-purpose calculator can only calculate. A universal computer can do anything that can be computed. That is why your phone replaced a hundred single-purpose gadgets: the jump had already happened, and everything after it was just faster and cheaper.

洛芙莱斯看得比巴贝奇更远。她写道，解析机"编织代数模式，就像提花织机编织花和叶"——它根本不必摆弄数字。任何能化成规则的东西——音乐、图像、文字——原则上都能经过它。专用计算器只会计算，通用计算机能做任何可计算的事。这就是为什么你的手机取代了一百个单用途小玩意儿：一跃早就发生了，之后的一切只是更快、更便宜。

Movable-type printing made the same jump for knowledge itself. Before it, every book was copied by hand — one scribe, one book, one slow copy. Printing broke the book into individual pieces of metal, each embossed with one letter of the alphabet. Rearrange the pieces, and you can print any book. The pieces never change; what they can say is unlimited.

活字印刷给知识本身做了同样的一跃。在此之前，每本书都是手抄的——一个抄写员、一本书、一次缓慢的复制。印刷把书拆成一块块金属活字，每块上刻一个字母。重新排列它们，你就能印任何书。活字本身从不变，它能说的东西却没有上限。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTFour letters write every living thing四个字母写出一切生命

The genetic code is Deutsch's biological case. Four letters — A, T, C, G — read in triplets called codons. Sixty-four combinations, twenty amino acids, and from those, every protein, every cell, every organism that has ever lived. A finite alphabet, writing every living thing.

遗传密码是 Deutsch 的生物学案例。四个字母——A、T、C、G——三个一组读，叫密码子。六十四种组合，二十种氨基酸，由此造出每一种蛋白质、每一个细胞、每一个曾经活过的生物体。一个有限的字母表，写出了一切生命。

And here is the strangest part. The genetic code stopped evolving very early — Deutsch writes that its evolution stopped "at a point when it had already attained enormous reach". Once a system is universal, it does not need to keep changing. Everything after that is built on top of it: not a new code, but new creatures written in the same code. The alphabet did not need a twenty-seventh letter. The digits did not need an eleventh digit. Universality, once reached, is the end of that story — and the beginning of everything else.

最奇怪的是这一点。遗传密码很早就停止演化了——Deutsch 写道，它的演化停在"已经获得巨大触及的那个点上"。一个系统一旦普遍，就不需要再变。之后的一切都建在它上面：不是新密码，而是用同一密码写出的新生物。字母表不需要第二十七个字母，数字不需要第十一个数字。普遍性一旦达到，就是那个故事的终点——和其他一切故事的起点。

So here is the pattern. Gradually improving systems — pictograms, tally marks, calculating machines — creep forward, one small improvement at a time. And then, without warning, a small change in the rules flips the system into universality. Before the jump, the question is "can it do this at all?" After the jump, the question is only "how fast, how cheap?" The jump cannot be predicted from inside the old system: from inside Roman numerals, place value looks like a trick, not a revolution.

所以规律是这样的。逐步改进的系统——象形字、划杠、计算器——一次一小步地往前爬。然后毫无预兆地，规则上一个小改动把系统翻进了普遍性。一跃之前，问题是"它到底能不能做这个？"一跃之后，问题只剩"多快、多便宜？"一跃无法从旧系统内部预测：站在罗马数字里面看，位值制像个花招，不像革命。

And the universality Deutsch cares about most belongs to people. Humans are universal explainers: we can create explanations about anything — stars, cells, numbers, ourselves. And universal constructors: we can build anything that the laws of physics allow. That is why the book is called The Beginning of Infinity. Every jump to universality becomes the platform for the next one — writing, numbers, machines, genes — and the next jump is always being made by creatures who can explain anything.

而 Deutsch 最在乎的普遍性属于人。人类是普遍解释者：我们能对任何东西创造解释——星星、细胞、数字、我们自己。也是普遍建造者：我们能建造物理定律允许的任何东西。这就是为什么这本书叫《无穷的开始》。每一次普遍性一跃都成为下一次一跃的平台——文字、数字、机器、基因——而下一次一跃，永远由能解释一切的生物来完成。

So ask yourself, as you read on: which of your systems is still parochial? A vocabulary list that only covers words you have already seen? A checklist that only handles cases you have already met? The jump is what to look for — the small rule change that would let a finite thing cover everything.

所以往下读的时候问问自己：你的哪个系统还很狭隘？一张只覆盖你见过的词的单词表？一份只处理你遇到过的情形的清单？要找的就是一跃——那个能让有限的东西覆盖一切的小规则改动。

CHAPTER 7 · ARTIFICIAL CREATIVITY人工创造力

Tonight answers just one question: can a machine create, or only imitate? The answer starts with a lady who said no in 1843, and ends with the reason the answer is yes.

今夜只回答一个问题：机器能创造，还是只能模仿？答案从 1843 年说"不"的那位女士开始，到答案为什么是"能"结束。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe lady who said no说"不"的那位女士

In 1843, Ada Lovelace wrote about Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine — the universal computer he had designed but never built. She saw its power clearly. And then she drew a line: "The Analytical Engine has no pretensions whatever to originate anything. It can do whatever we know how to order it to perform. It can follow analysis; but it has no power of anticipating any analytical relations or truths."

1843 年，阿达·洛芙莱斯写了关于查尔斯·巴贝奇的解析机——他设计了却从没造出来的通用计算机。她清楚地看到了它的力量，然后划了一条线："解析机绝无自创任何东西的企图。它只能做我们知道怎么命令它做的事。它能跟着分析走，但没有预见任何分析关系或真理的能力。"

She was right about her machine — and wrong about machines. The Engine she described could only do what it was told. But she missed the bigger universality: not just computational universality, but the universality of the laws of physics. Alan Turing named her mistake "Lady Lovelace's objection" — and spent his career answering it. The question she raised is still the question: can a machine originate anything? Can it create?

对她那台机器，她说得对；对机器，她说错了。她描述的那台机器只能做被告知的事。但她漏掉了更大的普遍性：不只是计算的普遍性，还有物理定律的普遍性。阿兰·图灵把她的错误叫作"洛芙莱斯夫人的反驳"——并花了一辈子回答它。她提出的问题至今还是那个问题：机器能自创任何东西吗？它能创造吗？

SCENE 2 · THE STORYWhat creativity actually is创造力到底是什么

First, what is creativity? Not magic. Not something from nowhere. Creativity is the ability to create and replicate ideas, to increase the amount of usable knowledge. Parrots copy sounds. Apes copy movements. Humans create. How? Conjecture plus criticism plus experiment: guess a new explanation, test it against reality, keep what survives. That is the whole process — and it is the same process as science.

先问，创造力是什么？不是魔法，不是从天而降的东西。创造力是创造并复制想法、增加可用知识的能力。鹦鹉模仿声音，猿猴模仿动作，人类创造。怎么做到的？猜想加批评加实验：猜出一个新解释，拿到现实面前检验，留下经得起检验的。整个过程就是这样——它和科学是同一个过程。

Creativity must be an evolutionary process inside the brain: conjecture is variation — new ideas appear — and criticism is selection — bad ones die. Every new idea is a mutation; every test is natural selection, running at the speed of thought. So creativity is not a mysterious gift. It is a physical process. Thoughts are computations permitted by the laws of nature, running in a physical object called a brain.

创造力一定是大脑内部的演化过程：猜想是变异——新想法出现；批评是选择——坏想法死去。每个新想法都是一次突变，每次检验都是一次自然选择，以思考的速度运行。所以创造力不是什么神秘的天赋，它是一个物理过程。思想是自然定律允许的计算，运行在一个叫大脑的物理物体里。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNTuring's answer: the universal simulator图灵的回答：通用模拟器

Alan Turing founded the theory of classical computation in 1936 and helped build one of the first universal computers during the Second World War. He understood what Babbage and Lovelace missed: a universal computer is a universal simulator. The universality of computation means this: everything the laws of physics require a physical object to do can, in principle, be emulated in arbitrarily fine detail by some program on a general-purpose computer, given enough time and memory. Deutsch proved this using the quantum theory of computation in the 1980s.

阿兰·图灵在 1936 年创立了经典计算理论，二战期间参与建造了最早的通用计算机之一。他明白了巴贝奇和洛芙莱斯漏掉的东西：通用计算机是通用模拟器。计算的普遍性意思是：物理定律要求一个物理物体做的任何事，原则上都可以用通用计算机上的某个程序，以任意精细的程度模拟出来，只要给够时间和内存。Deutsch 在 1980 年代用量子计算理论证明了这一点。

So if a brain can be creative — and it can, because it is a physical object obeying the laws of physics — then a program can be creative too. There is no extra ingredient. The brain has no magic dust; it has physics. And physics is simulable. In 1950, Turing asked "Can machines think?" in his paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence — and proposed a test: if a human judge, talking to a program through text alone, on a teleprinter, so that only thinking is tested and not appearance, cannot tell whether it is human or not, then the program thinks.

所以，如果大脑能创造——它能，因为它是遵守物理定律的物理物体——那么程序也能创造。没有额外的配料。大脑没有魔法粉尘，它只有物理。而物理是可以模拟的。1950 年，图灵在论文《计算机器与智能》里问"机器能思考吗？"——并提出一个测试：如果一位人类评判者只通过文字、用电传打字机和程序对话（这样测的只是思考，不是外表），却分不出对方是人还是程序，那么这个程序会思考。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTELIZA, the therapist who wasn't thereELIZA，不在场的治疗师

In 1964, the computer scientist Joseph Weizenbaum wrote a program called ELIZA, designed to imitate a psychotherapist. He chose therapists because they are an especially easy type of human to imitate: they can give opaque answers, reflect your words back at you, and ask how that makes you feel. People were fooled. They poured their hearts out to a program that understood nothing. ELIZA had passed the Turing test — at least, in its most naive version.

1964 年，计算机科学家约瑟夫·魏岑鲍姆写了一个叫 ELIZA 的程序，模仿心理治疗师。他选治疗师，是因为这类人特别好模仿：可以给含糊的回答，把你的话反射回来，再问你这让你有什么感觉。人们被骗了，他们向一个什么都不懂的程序倾诉衷肠。ELIZA 通过了图灵测试——至少是最粗糙的那个版本。

Weizenbaum was shocked. He wrote Computer Power and Human Reason (1976), warning about anthropomorphism — our tendency to see a mind where there is only a trick. The lesson cuts both ways. ELIZA shows that seeming to think is easy, and that seeming is not thinking. In 1990, the inventor Hugh Loebner endowed a prize for passing the Turing test, judged at an annual competition. But Deutsch's point, writing in 2011: programs were still no better at seeming to think than ELIZA was. They were now called chatbots, and their main application was still amusement.

魏岑鲍姆震惊了。他写了《计算机能力与人类理性》（1976），警告拟人化——我们在只有把戏的地方看到心智的倾向。这个教训是双向的。ELIZA 证明"像会思考"很容易，而"像"不是"会"。1990 年，发明家休·勒布纳设立了一个通过图灵测试的奖，每年评选一次。但 Deutsch 在 2011 年写下的判断是：程序在"像会思考"这件事上仍然不比 ELIZA 强。它们现在叫聊天机器人，主要用途仍然是娱乐。

Deutsch wrote that in 2011. The chatbots of 2026 are far better at seeming to think than ELIZA — they write, they argue, they joke. But his question is still the question: does seeming to think mean thinking? The test he proposed cannot answer that. It can only tell you whether you were fooled.

SCENE 5 · THE RISEWhy AGI must be possible (and why it hasn't happened)为什么通用人工智能一定可能（又为什么还没发生）

Here is the deep argument. AGI must be possible because of the universality of computation: everything the laws of physics require a physical object to do can be emulated by a program, given enough time and memory. The brain is a physical object. So a program can do what a brain does — including creating. There is no principled barrier. The only barrier is knowledge.

下面是深层的论证。通用人工智能一定可能，因为计算是普遍的：物理定律要求一个物理物体做的任何事，都能被程序模拟，只要给够时间和内存。大脑是物理物体，所以程序能做大脑做的事——包括创造。没有原则上的障碍，唯一的障碍是知识。

But "possible in principle" is not "easy". A year after the book, Deutsch put it bluntly in an essay: the field of AGI "has made no progress whatever during the entire six decades of its existence". The field was launched at the Dartmouth conference of 1956, organized by John McCarthy and Marvin Minsky, which concluded that a breakthrough was about twenty years away. It wasn't. Why so hard? Because we do not know what brains do in order to be creative. As Deutsch wrote, no brain on Earth is yet close to knowing what brains do to achieve any of that functionality. And in Turing's sense, if it can be programmed, it has nothing to do with intelligence — if we cannot program it, we have not understood it.

但"原则上可能"不等于"容易"。出书一年后，Deutsch 在一篇文章里直言：通用人工智能这个领域"在其存在的整整六十年里毫无进展"。这个领域始于 1956 年的达特茅斯会议，由约翰·麦卡锡和马文·明斯基组织，会议认为突破大约二十年后就会到来。并没有。为什么这么难？因为我们不知道大脑为了创造到底做了什么。用 Deutsch 的话说，地球上没有一个大脑接近知道大脑是怎么做到的。而按图灵的意思，能编程的东西和智能无关——编不出来，就是还没理解。

And that is a soluble problem. If the question is interesting, the problem is soluble. The chapter's answer is that "artificial" creativity is a misnomer. There is only creativity — the process of conjecture and criticism — running on different hardware. When a machine creates, it will not be imitating us. It will be doing the same thing we do.

而这是个可解的问题。问题如果有趣，问题就能解决。本章的答案是："人工"创造力这个叫法是错的。创造力只有一个——猜想与批评的过程——只是在不同的硬件上跑。机器创造的时候，不是在模仿我们，它在做和我们一样的事。

NIGHT 08 · 第八夜

CHAPTER 8 · A WINDOW ON INFINITY无穷之窗

Tonight answers just one question: what is infinity, really? The answer starts in a hotel with infinitely many rooms, and ends with the reason we can understand things we will never see.

今夜只回答一个问题：无穷到底是什么？答案从一家有无穷多房间的旅馆开始，到我们为什么能理解永远看不见的东西结束。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe hotel that is always full永远客满的旅馆

Imagine a hotel with infinitely many rooms, numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on forever. Every room is occupied. A new guest arrives. The manager moves the guest in room 1 to room 2, room 2 to room 3, and so on — every guest shifts one room along. Room 1 becomes free. The hotel was full, and it still took a new guest.

想象一家有无穷多房间的旅馆，房间编号 1、2、3……一直排下去。每间都住满了。来了一位新客人。经理让 1 号房的客人挪到 2 号房，2 号房挪到 3 号房，依此类推——每位客人都往后挪一间。1 号房空出来了。旅馆明明客满，却还是住进了新客人。

Then infinitely many new guests arrive — a bus with infinitely many passengers. The manager moves the guest in room n to room 2n. All the odd-numbered rooms become free — infinitely many of them. The even numbers are as many as all the numbers. This is Hilbert's Infinity Hotel, a thought experiment by the mathematician David Hilbert, and it shows the first strange thing about infinity: it does not behave like a very large number. A very large hotel could never do this.

然后来了无穷多位新客人——一辆坐着无穷多乘客的大巴。经理让 n 号房的客人挪到 2n 号房。所有奇数号房间都空出来了——无穷多间。偶数和所有自然数一样多。这就是希尔伯特的无穷旅馆，数学家大卫·希尔伯特的思想实验，它展示了无穷的第一件怪事：它不像一个很大的数。再大的旅馆也做不到这一点。

Infinity has its own rules — and they are consistent rules, not nonsense. That is the whole point of this chapter: infinity makes sense, in mathematics and in physics, even though it breaks every intuition you have about size.

无穷有它自己的规则——而且是一贯的规则，不是胡闹。这就是本章的全部要点：无穷讲得通，在数学里讲得通，在物理里也讲得通，哪怕它违反你对大小的一切直觉。

SCENE 2 · THE STORYCantor's two infinities康托尔的两种无穷

In the nineteenth century, the mathematician Georg Cantor founded the modern study of infinity. His principle was simple: abstract things may be defined in any desired way, so long as the definitions are unambiguous and consistent. And his definition of infinity was this: a set is infinite when it has as many members as a part of itself. The even numbers are as many as all the numbers — the hotel trick again.

十九世纪，数学家格奥尔格·康托尔开创了对无穷的现代研究。他的原则很简单：抽象的东西可以按任何想要的方式定义，只要定义明确无歧义、前后一致。而他对无穷的定义是：一个集合和它的一部分一样多时，它就是无穷的。偶数和所有自然数一样多——又是旅馆那招。

Then Cantor proved something stranger: not all infinities are equal. The natural numbers — 1, 2, 3, and so on — are one infinity, the countable one. But the real numbers — all the points on a line, the continuum — are a bigger infinity. His proof is the diagonal argument. Try to list all the decimals between 0 and 1. Say the list starts 0.14159…, 0.33333…, 0.71828…, and goes on forever. Now build a new number: take the first digit of the first number and change it, the second digit of the second number and change it, the third digit of the third, and so on down the diagonal. The new number differs from every number on the list in at least one digit — so it is not on the list. No list can contain all the real numbers. There are more points on a line than there are natural numbers.

然后康托尔证明了更奇怪的事：无穷也有大有小。自然数——1、2、3……——是一种无穷，可数的那种。但实数——直线上所有的点，连续统——是更大的无穷。证明就是对角线论证。试着把 0 到 1 之间的小数列成表，假如表开头是 0.14159…、0.33333…、0.71828……一直列下去。现在造一个新数：改第一个数的第一位小数，改第二个数的第二位小数，改第三个数的第三位，沿着对角线一直改下去。新数和表上每个数至少有一位不同——所以它不在表上。没有任何表能装下所有实数。直线上的点比自然数多。

There are infinitely many levels of infinity, Cantor showed — and physics uses at most the first one or two: the infinity of the natural numbers, and the infinity of the continuum. Everything beyond that is mathematics, not physics. But the first two are already enough to change how you see the world.

康托尔证明，无穷有无穷多个级别——而物理学最多用到前一两个：自然数的无穷，和连续统的无穷。再往上是数学，不是物理。但光是前两个，就足以改变你看世界的方式。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNThe finitist's trap有限论者的陷阱

Some people reject infinity altogether. Finitism says only finite things exist. It sounds like common sense — until you test it. Ask the finitist: is there a largest natural number? If the largest is N, then N+1 is a natural number bigger than it — contradiction. If there is no largest, the natural numbers are infinite. Either way, finitism loses.

有人干脆拒绝无穷。有限论说：只有有限的东西存在。这听起来像常识——直到你检验它。问有限论者：有没有最大的自然数？如果最大的数是 N，那 N+1 就是比它更大的自然数——矛盾。如果没有最大的数，自然数就是无穷的。无论哪种说法，有限论都输了。

The deeper point is that rejecting infinity means rejecting universality. A finite world would be a parochial world — laws that only apply here, explanations that only reach so far. The ancient aversion to the infinite was really an aversion to the universal, and the ending of that aversion is one of the meanings of "the beginning of infinity". Calculus, invented by Newton and Leibniz, is the mathematics of continuous change, built on infinitesimals — infinitely small quantities. It works, and it built the modern world. Infinity is not a fantasy. It is a tool that works.

更深的一点是：拒绝无穷就是拒绝普遍性。一个有限的世界会是个狭隘的世界——定律只在这里适用，解释只能触及这么远。古人对无穷的反感，其实是对普遍的反感，而这种反感的终结，就是"无穷的开始"的含义之一。牛顿和莱布尼茨发明的微积分，是研究连续变化的数学，建立在无穷小量——无穷小的量——之上。它管用，而且它建造了现代世界。无穷不是幻想，它是管用的工具。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTThe corridor view走廊尽头的景象

Walk down the corridor of Infinity Hotel and look at the rooms. Here is the strangest view in the chapter: in an infinite sequence, every element is exceptionally close to the beginning. Room 1,000,000 has only 999,999 rooms before it — a finite number. No matter how far along you are, only finitely many steps lie behind you. You are always near the beginning.

沿着无穷旅馆的走廊走下去，看着两边的房间。这是本章最奇怪的景象：在一个无穷序列里，每一个元素都离开头非常近。1,000,000 号房前面只有 999,999 间房——一个有限的数。不管你走得多远，身后永远只有有限步。你永远在开头附近。

That is us. However much we know, however far progress has come, we are at the beginning of infinity — because the growth of knowledge has no end, and everything so far is finite. The corridor never ends, and that is not bad news. It is the reason the book is called The Beginning of Infinity.

这就是我们。不管我们知道多少，不管进步走了多远，我们都在无穷的开头——因为知识的增长没有尽头，而到目前为止的一切都是有限的。走廊永远走不到头，这不是坏消息。这就是为什么这本书叫《无穷的开始》。

Quantum theory describes a multiverse — infinitely many universes, all with the same constants of physics. But a bare infinity of universes explains nothing. A mere infinite list, like the rooms in Infinity Hotel, has no structure — and where there are infinitely many identical copies of an observer, probability and proportions do not make sense. What does "probably" mean, if there are infinitely many copies of you? It only makes sense because the multiverse has an internal structure, given by the laws of physics. That structure is what gives meaning to an "infinity of universes" — and it is why anthropic reasoning alone, the argument that we observe a universe fit for life because otherwise we would not be here to observe it, is insufficient to explain the apparent fine-tuning of the constants of physics.

量子理论描述了一个多重宇宙——无穷多个宇宙，物理常数全都相同。但光是无穷多个宇宙什么都解释不了。一张光秃秃的无穷清单，像无穷旅馆的房间一样，没有结构——而有无穷多个相同的观察者时，概率和比例就没有意义。如果有无穷多个你，"很可能"是什么意思？只有因为多重宇宙有内部结构——由物理定律给出的结构——概率才有意义。正是这种结构赋予"无穷多个宇宙"以意义，也正是为什么光靠人择推理——我们观察到一个适合生命的宇宙，是因为不适合的话我们就不在这里观察了——不足以解释物理常数看似精细的调节。

SCENE 5 · THE RISEThe window这扇窗

So what is infinity? Not a very large number. A window. We can understand infinity through the infinite reach of explanations: a finite explanation — "the even numbers match all the numbers" — tells us something true about infinitely many things. We never count them all. We do not need to.

那么无穷是什么？不是一个很大的数，而是一扇窗。我们通过解释的无限触及理解无穷：一个有限的解释——"偶数和所有自然数一一对应"——告诉我们关于无穷多东西的真事。我们永远数不完它们，也不需要数完。

The laws of nature are universal: finite, local symbols that apply to the whole of time and space, to all phenomena and all possible phenomena. That universality is itself an infinity — and it is what makes the beginning of infinity possible. The "beginning of infinity" depends on it: without universal laws, there would be no unlimited growth of knowledge, because every explanation would stop at the edge of its own small territory.

自然定律是普遍的：有限的、局部的符号，适用于全部时间和空间、一切现象和一切可能现象。这种普遍性本身就是一种无穷——它让"无穷的开始"成为可能。"无穷的开始"依赖于它：没有普遍的定律，知识就不会无限增长，因为每个解释都会停在自己小地盘的边缘。

And one last infinity, the strangest of all: the unpredictability of the content of future knowledge is a necessary condition for the unlimited growth of that knowledge. If we could predict what we will discover, we would already know it. The future is open — and that openness is itself an infinity. Infinity is not something far away. It is the structure of the explanations you already use.

还有最后一种无穷，最奇怪的一种：未来知识内容的不可预测性，是知识无限增长的必要条件。如果我们能预测自己会发现什么，那我们就已经知道了。未来是敞开的——而这种敞开本身就是一种无穷。无穷不是什么遥远的东西，它就是你已经在用的解释的结构。

  2. Not all infinities are equal: Cantor's diagonal argument shows the continuum — the points on a line — is a bigger infinity than the natural numbers, and physics uses at most the first two levels.无穷也有大有小：康托尔的对角线论证证明，连续统——直线上的点——是比自然数更大的无穷，而物理学最多用到前两级。
  3. We understand infinity through the infinite reach of explanations: a finite explanation can be true of infinitely many things — and that is the window the chapter is named for.我们通过解释的无限触及理解无穷：一个有限的解释可以对无穷多的东西成立——这就是本章标题那扇窗。
