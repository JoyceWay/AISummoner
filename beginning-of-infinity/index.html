NIGHT 01 · 第一夜

CHAPTER 1 · THE REACH OF EXPLANATIONS解释的 reach

Tonight answers just one question: what does a good explanation look like? The answer hides in a goddess and a tilted axis.

今夜只回答一个问题：好解释长什么样？答案藏在一个女神和一根倾斜的地轴里。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGNo one has been to a star没有人去过星星

No one has ever been to a star. No one has touched one, no one has drilled into one to look inside.

没有人去过一颗星星。没有人摸过它，没有人钻进它的内部看一眼。

And yet astronomers know why stars shine: nuclear fusion. Hydrogen becomes helium, mass becomes light.

可天文学家知道星星为什么发光：核聚变。氢变成氦，质量变成光。

How do they know? Not by looking harder — the senses can't reach there. They know because they have an explanation that fits.

他们是怎么知道的？不是靠"看得更仔细"——感官到不了那里。靠的是解释。

This is the starting point of the whole book, and Deutsch's reversal of common sense: knowledge doesn't get "read out" of the senses. Empiricism says theories come from sense reports — we see things, then generalize. Deutsch says no. Theories are guessed, then criticized, picked apart, replaced. The senses are the judge, not the author.

这就是整本书的起点，也是 Deutsch 对常识的一次翻转：知识不是从感官里"读"出来的。经验主义说，理论来自感官报告——我们看到什么，就总结出什么。Deutsch 说，不对。理论是猜出来的，然后被批评、被挑刺、被改掉。感官只是裁判，不是作者。

So the question becomes: how do you tell a good guess from a bad one?

所以问题变成：猜出来的东西，怎么分好坏？

SCENE 2 · THE STORYThe goddess's winter女神的冬天

Long ago, the Greeks also had to explain winter. They told this story:

很久以前，希腊人也要解释冬天。他们讲了这样一个故事：

Hades, king of the underworld, kidnapped Persephone, goddess of spring. Her mother Demeter, goddess of the earth, grieved, and negotiated: her daughter must return to the underworld for part of each year. And in the days when her daughter was gone, Demeter's sadness made the earth cold — that was winter.

冥王哈迪斯绑走了春之女神珀耳塞福涅。她的母亲、地母得墨忒耳悲痛欲绝，与哈迪斯谈判：女儿每年必须回冥界住一段时间。而在女儿离开的日子里，得墨忒耳的悲伤让大地变冷——那就是冬天。

Is the story beautiful? Yes. Does it explain winter? It seems to.

这个故事美吗？美。能解释冬天吗？好像能。

And — here's the key — the story is testable. If winter is caused by Demeter's grief, winter should fall on the whole earth at once. But Australians will tell you: in December, it's summer there. The story is refuted.

而且——关键来了——这个故事是可以被检验的。如果冬天是得墨忒耳的悲伤造成的，那冬天应该同时降临整个大地。可澳大利亚人会告诉你：十二月，他们那里是夏天。故事被证伪了。

So is "testable" the standard of a good explanation? Deutsch says: no. That's the real turn of the first night.

所以"可检验"就是好解释的标准吗？Deutsch 说：不是。这才是第一夜真正的转折。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNA story fixed with one sentence一句话就能修好的故事

Suppose a Greek heard about summer in the southern hemisphere. Would he have to abandon the myth? No. One sentence fixes it:

假设一个希腊人听说了南半球的夏天。他需要抛弃这个神话吗？不需要。他一句话就能修好它：

"When Demeter is sad she banishes warmth from her vicinity, and it has to go elsewhere — into the southern hemisphere."
得墨忒耳悲伤时，把温暖从她身边驱逐出去——温暖无处可去，只好跑到南半球。

Fixed. Winter in the north, summer in the south, the story still works. Swap the god, swap the motive, add a clause — change it any way you like.

修好了。冬天在北半球，夏天在南半球，故事依然成立。换一个神、换一个动机、加一个条款——怎么改都行。

That is the true face of a bad explanation: easy to vary. Deutsch's definition at the end of the chapter:

这就是坏解释的真面目：easy to vary（容易变通）。Deutsch 在章末的定义是：

A bad explanation is one "that is easy to vary while still accounting for what it purports to account for."
坏解释就是那种"随便改改，还能解释它要解释的东西"的解释。

An explanation that can explain anything actually explains nothing. If it can explain green rainbows, weekly seasons, or no seasons at all — then it hasn't explained winter.

一个解释如果能解释任何东西，那它其实什么都没解释。能解释绿色彩虹、每周一次的四季、或者根本没有四季——那它就没有解释冬天。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTThe tilted axis倾斜的地轴

Now the real explanation: the Earth's axis is tilted. As the Earth orbits the sun, each hemisphere leans toward the sun for half the year and away for the other half. Leaning toward is summer; leaning away is winter.

现在看真正的解释：地轴是倾斜的。地球绕太阳转时，每个半球有半年朝向太阳，半年背向太阳。朝向太阳就是夏天，背向就是冬天。

Try changing it. Swap the sun for the moon? No — the moon's position in the sky doesn't repeat once a year, while the seasons do. Remove "tilted surfaces receive less heat"? No — the whole explanation collapses. Remove the tilt? Then there are no seasons.

试着改它。把太阳换成月亮？不行——月亮在天空中的位置一年不重复一次，而四季是一年一循环。把"倾斜的表面受热更少"这句去掉？不行——那整个解释就塌了。把倾斜改成不倾斜？那就没有四季了。

Every detail is doing work; change one, and the explanation stops explaining. That is hard to vary:

每一个细节都在干活，改一个，解释就停止解释。这就是 hard to vary（很难变通）：

That is a good explanation — hard to vary, because all its details play a functional role.
这就是好解释——很难变通，因为它的每一个细节都在起功能作用。

And these details are known independently: we can verify in a lab, without waiting for winter, that a tilted surface is heated less; we know without watching the seasons that a spinning sphere in space points in a constant direction. The parts of the explanation come from elsewhere; assembled, they happen to explain the seasons.

而且这些细节是独立知道的：我们不用等冬天就能在实验室里验证"倾斜的表面受热更少"，不用看四季就知道"旋转的球体在空间中指向恒定"。解释的零件来自别处，拼在一起，恰好解释了四季。

By the way: precisely because this explanation is hard to vary, it takes a risk. If December in England had the same weather as December in Australia, the whole theory would have to be thrown out — no small repairs allowed. Good explanations put themselves in danger like that.

顺便说一句：正因为这个解释很难变通，所以它冒着风险。如果十二月的英格兰和十二月的澳大利亚天气一样，这个理论就得整个扔掉，不能小修小补。好解释就是这样——它把自己置于危险之中。

SCENE 5 · THE RISEReach: an explanation travels farther than its birthplaceReach：解释走得比出生地更远

The tilted axis doesn't just explain Earth's seasons. It predicts other things: the southern hemisphere's seasons must be opposite the northern; Mars (axial tilt about 25°, close to Earth's 23.5°) must have seasons too; the poles must have half a year of day and half a year of night.

地轴倾斜不只解释了地球的四季。它还预测了别的东西：南半球的四季必须和北半球相反；火星（地轴倾角约 25°，和地球的 23.5° 接近）也必须有四季；两极必须有半年白天、半年黑夜。

None of these were problems the explanation was "born" to solve. It was born to explain Earth's winter, and it went on to explain Mars and predict the southern hemisphere. Deutsch calls this capacity reach:

这些都不是这个解释"出生"时要解决的问题。它生来解释地球的冬天，却顺手解释了火星、预测了南半球。Deutsch 把这种能力叫做 reach：

A good explanation's reach is infinite. A correct explanation doesn't only apply to the circumstances it was created for — in principle it applies to the whole universe. Demeter's grief explains nothing but winter, and even winter badly. The tilted axis: one explanation, explains wherever it goes.

好解释的 reach 是无限的。一个正确的解释不只适用于它出生的情境，原则上适用于整个宇宙。得墨忒耳的悲伤除了冬天什么都解释不了——连冬天都解释得很糟。而地轴倾斜，一个解释，走到哪里解释到哪里。

This is the first layer of "infinity" in the book's title: good explanations have no boundary.

这就是书名"无穷的开始"里"无穷"的第一层意思：好解释没有边界。

We do not test every testable theory, but only the few that we find are good explanations.
我们并不是检验每一个可检验的理论，而只检验那些我们认为是好解释的少数理论。

Testing is not the starting point of science. First comes a good explanation; only then is it worth testing.

检验不是科学的起点。先有好解释，才值得检验。

NIGHT 02 · 第二夜

CHAPTER 2 · CLOSER TO REALITY更接近实在

Tonight answers just one question: explanations are guesses — so how do guesses get closer and closer to being right? The answer starts with a student, a photographic plate, and a galaxy that wasn't there.

今夜只回答一个问题：解释都是猜的，猜怎么会越猜越对？答案从一个学生、一张底片、一个不存在的星系开始。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe student who counted galaxies数星系的学生

As a graduate student, Deutsch was once shown the Palomar Sky Survey — dramatic photographs of clusters of galaxies, fuzzy shapes against the black, set against the sharp points of nearby stars. The scale was overwhelming: whole galaxies, each holding billions of stars, reduced to smudges on a plate.

做研究生时，Deutsch 有一次看到了帕洛玛巡天（Palomar Sky Survey）的照片——成团的星系，模糊的光斑，对比附近恒星锐利的光点。尺度大得吓人：整个星系，几十亿颗星星，在底片上只剩一团模糊。

His job was to catalogue those galaxies — to look at photographic plates, by eye, and decide what was a galaxy and what was not. The tools were rudimentary. The work was slow. And it was, Deutsch says, creative and fun — not mindless toil.

他的工作是给这些星系编目——用肉眼看底片，判断什么是星系、什么不是。工具很原始，工作很慢。但 Deutsch 说，这工作有创造性，而且好玩——不是无脑苦力。

Already, a question is hiding in this scene: when you "just look" at a plate, are you seeing reality — or are you already interpreting it?

这个场景里已经藏了一个问题：当你"只是看看"底片时，你看到的是实在，还是已经在解读它了？

SCENE 2 · THE STORYThe galaxy that wasn't there不存在的星系

One day, a student mistook a flaw in the photograph for a galaxy. A smudge on the plate — a defect in the emulsion, a speck of dust — looked exactly like a distant galaxy. It was catalogued. For a while, it was a galaxy.

有一天，一个学生把底片上的一个瑕疵当成了星系。底片上的一团污迹——感光层的一个缺陷、一粒灰尘——看起来和一个遥远的星系一模一样。它被编进了目录。有一阵子，它就是一个星系。

Whose mistake was it? Not the eyes — the eyes saw the smudge correctly. The mistake was in the theory: the interpretation that said "this smudge is a galaxy." The error lived in the explanation, not in the observation.

这是谁的错？不是眼睛的错——眼睛正确地看到了那团污迹。错在理论里：那个"这团污迹是个星系"的解读。错误住在解释里，不住在观察里。

That is the first crack in a very old idea: that knowledge comes from looking. If looking were enough, a smudge could never become a galaxy. But it did — because looking is never just looking.

这就是一个古老想法的第一道裂缝：知识来自"看"。如果"看"就够了，一团污迹永远变不成星系。可它变成了——因为"看"从来不只是"看"。

The growth of knowledge consists of correcting misconceptions in our theories.
知识的增长，就是纠正我们理论中的误解。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNYou never see anything directly你从未直接看到任何东西

Here is Deutsch's blunt claim, the sentence this whole night turns on:

下面是 Deutsch 直白的论断，今夜整章都绕着这句话转：

We observe nothing directly anyway. All observation is theory-laden.
反正我们什么都不是直接观察到的。所有的观察都负载着理论。

Theory-laden: every observation already carries a theory inside it. Your eyes don't hand you the world raw. They hand you an interpretation — and your brain builds the rest.

Theory-laden（负载理论）：每一次观察里面都已经装着一个理论。你的眼睛递给你的不是原生的世界，而是一个解读——剩下的由你的大脑补完。

We never perceive anything directly. All our external experience is virtual reality... generated by our own brains.
我们从未直接感知任何东西。我们所有的外部经验都是虚拟现实……由我们自己的大脑生成。

Read that again: virtual reality. Not as a metaphor for headsets — as a description of ordinary seeing. What you call "seeing the world" is your brain running a simulation, built from signals, corrected by theories you already hold. The world out there is real. But the picture in your head is a construction.

再读一遍：虚拟现实。不是指头盔的比喻，而是对日常"看"的描述。你说的"看到世界"，是大脑在跑一个模拟——用信号搭建，用你已有的理论修正。外面的世界是真实的，但你脑子里的画面是建构出来的。

That is why appearances can be deceptive. And it is also why we — and our instruments — can correct for that deceptiveness. A deception you can correct is not a wall. It is a door.

所以表象会骗人。也正因为如此，我们——和我们的仪器——才能纠正这种骗人。能被纠正的欺骗不是一堵墙，而是一扇门。

That is why appearances can be deceptive, and it is also why we, and our instruments, can correct for that deceptiveness.
这就是为什么表象会骗人，也正是为什么我们——和我们的仪器——能够纠正这种骗人。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTThe telescope brings you closer望远镜让你更近

Now the obvious worry: if all observation is interpretation, and instruments add even more layers of it — telescopes, detectors, computers processing data — then surely instruments take us farther from reality, not closer. Astronomers don't even look at the sky any more. They stare at data.

现在一个明显的担心来了：如果所有观察都是解读，而仪器又加了更多层解读——望远镜、探测器、电脑处理数据——那仪器岂不是让我们离实在更远，而不是更近？天文学家现在根本不看天了，只盯着数据。

Deutsch says: exactly backwards. It may seem strange —

Deutsch 说：正好反了。这听起来很奇怪——

It may seem strange that scientific instruments bring us closer to reality when in purely physical terms they only ever separate us further from it.
科学仪器让我们更接近实在，这听起来可能很奇怪——因为从纯物理的角度说，它们只会让我们离实在更远。

But "closer" was never about physical distance. Your naked eye tells you a star is a white dot. A telescope tells you it is a massive nuclear furnace, millions of kilometres across. Which one is closer to reality? The telescope — because it corrects the error your eye makes.

但"更近"从来不是物理距离的问题。你的肉眼告诉你，星星是一个白点。望远镜告诉你，它是一个巨大的核熔炉，直径几百万公里。哪一个更接近实在？望远镜——因为它纠正了你的眼睛犯的错。

Telescopes... are rungs on a ladder of theoretical interpretation which reaches down to the true, unseen reality.
望远镜……是理论解读之梯上的一级，通向真实的、看不见的实在。

Think of it as a ladder. Each rung is a better theory. Your eye is one rung. The telescope is a higher rung — a theory built into glass and metal, tested and corrected. You climb the ladder not by getting physically nearer, but by getting the interpretation righter.

把它想成一架梯子。每一级都是一个更好的理论。你的眼睛是一级，望远镜是更高的一级——一个用玻璃和金属造出来的理论，经过检验和修正。你爬梯子靠的不是物理上更近，而是解读得更对。

So the astronomer who never looks at the sky is not farther from reality. She is closer — because she is using a better ladder.

所以那个从不看天的天文学家，离实在不是更远，而是更近——因为她用的梯子更好。

SCENE 5 · THE RISEKnowledge grows by correcting mistakes知识靠纠错生长

Put the pieces together and you get the engine of the whole book. Knowledge does not grow by collecting observations. It grows by correcting misconceptions.

把这些拼起来，就是整本书的引擎。知识不是靠收集观察来增长的，而是靠纠正误解来增长的。

The growth of knowledge consists of correcting misconceptions in our theories.
知识的增长，就是纠正我们理论中的误解。

Each correction moves the theory a little closer to reality. There is no ceiling to this: reality is comprehensible, and our error-correcting has no built-in stopping point. That is the second layer of "infinity" in the title — our explanations can approach reality without end.

每一次纠正，都让理论离实在近一点。这没有天花板：实在是可以被理解的，而我们的纠错没有内置的终点。这就是书名"无穷"的第二层意思——我们的解释可以无止境地接近实在。

And the work itself is not drudgery. Edison said research is "one per cent inspiration and ninety-nine per cent perspiration." Deutsch says that is misleading: people can apply creativity even to tasks that machines do uncreatively. Cataloguing galaxies by eye was repetitive — and it was creative, and fun. The toil can be creative, just as the discovery is.

而且这工作本身不是苦役。爱迪生说，研究是"百分之一的灵感加百分之九十九的汗水"。Deutsch 说，这话误导人：人即使在机器只会机械重复的任务里，也能发挥创造性。用肉眼给星系编目是重复的——同时它是有创造性的、好玩的。苦力也可以有创造性，和发现一样。

So: guesses get closer to being right not by piling up more observations, but by finding the misconceptions inside the guess and correcting them. That is how a smudge stops being a galaxy — and how a white dot becomes a furnace.

所以：猜越猜越对，靠的不是堆更多观察，而是找到猜里面的误解、纠正它。一团污迹就是这样不再是星系的，一个白点就是这样变成熔炉的。
