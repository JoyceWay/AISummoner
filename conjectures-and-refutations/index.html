NIGHT 01 · 第一夜

CHAPTER 1 · SCIENCE: CONJECTURES AND REFUTATIONS科学：猜想与反驳

Tonight answers just one question: when should a theory be called scientific? The answer starts in Vienna in 1919, with four famous theories — and ends with a test any theory must pass.

今夜只回答一个问题：一个理论什么时候才算科学？答案从 1919 年的维也纳开始——四个著名理论——到一个任何理论都必须通过的检验结束。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGVienna, 1919维也纳，1919 年

In the autumn of 1919, a young Karl Popper in Vienna had a problem. Not "when is a theory true?" — a harder question: "when should a theory be ranked as scientific?" He knew science often gets things wrong, and that a pseudoscience might sometimes stumble on the truth. So truth could not be the test. Something else had to draw the line.

1919 年秋天，维也纳的年轻卡尔·波普尔遇到了一个问题。不是"理论什么时候为真？"——而是更难的问题："一个理论什么时候才算科学？"他知道科学常常犯错，也知道伪科学有时会碰巧说对。所以"真"不能是标准，必须有别的东西来划这条线。

Four theories filled the air around him. The first was Einstein's theory of relativity — the most important of the four, in Popper's eyes. The other three were Marx's theory of history, Freud's psychoanalysis, and Alfred Adler's "individual psychology." All four had passionate followers. All four claimed to explain the world. And yet Popper felt they were not the same kind of thing at all.

他周围有四个理论。第一个是爱因斯坦的相对论——在波普尔眼里，这是四个里最重要的。另外三个是马克思的历史理论、弗洛伊德的精神分析，和阿尔弗雷德·阿德勒的"个体心理学"。四个理论都有狂热的追随者，都宣称能解释世界。可波普尔觉得，它们根本不是同一种东西。

This chapter is the story of that feeling — how a young man in Vienna turned an unease into a criterion, and how that criterion became the most famous idea in the philosophy of science. The chapter began as a lecture Popper gave at Peterhouse, Cambridge, in the summer of 1953, looking back on a question he had carried since 1919.

这一章讲的就是这种感觉的故事——维也纳的一个年轻人，如何把一种不安变成一条标准，而这条标准又如何成了科学哲学里最著名的思想。本章源于波普尔 1953 年夏天在剑桥彼得学院的一场讲座，回望他自 1919 年起就带着的那个问题。

SCENE 2 · THE STORYFour theories, one puzzle四个理论，一个谜

Start with Einstein. His theory of gravitation predicted something strange: light from a distant star, passing close to the sun, should bend — pulled by gravity, just as material bodies are pulled. In 1919, Arthur Eddington led an eclipse expedition to test it. During a total solar eclipse, the sun's light is blocked, and stars near the sun become visible. If their apparent positions had shifted by the amount Einstein predicted, the theory survived. If not, it fell.

先看爱因斯坦。他的引力理论预言了一件奇怪的事：遥远恒星的光经过太阳附近时应该弯曲——像物质受引力拉扯一样受引力拉扯。1919 年，亚瑟·爱丁顿率领一次日食远征队去检验它。日全食时太阳光被遮住，太阳附近的星星变得可见。如果它们的视位置移动了爱因斯坦预言的量，理论就活下来了；如果没有，理论就倒了。

The result thrilled Popper's whole circle of students: the light bent, as predicted. But notice what impressed Popper. It was not that Einstein was "proved right." It was that Einstein had taken a risk. His theory forbade certain outcomes. It could have failed — and everybody knew exactly what failure would look like.

结果让波普尔身边的整个学生圈子都激动了：光弯曲了，和预言的一样。但注意，真正打动波普尔的不是爱因斯坦"被证明是对的"，而是爱因斯坦冒了险。他的理论禁止了某些结果，它本可能失败——而且每个人都知道失败会是什么样子。

Now the other three. Their followers, Popper noticed, were impressed by the opposite thing: apparent explanatory power. A Marxist could find class struggle in every newspaper. A Freudian could explain any human behaviour — a man who drowns a child, and a man who saves a child, both "prove" the theory, one by one mechanism and the other by another. Adler's followers could do the same with any case at all.

再看另外三个。波普尔注意到，它们的追随者恰恰为相反的东西着迷：表面上的解释力。马克思主义者能在每份报纸里找到阶级斗争；弗洛伊德主义者能解释任何人类行为——一个淹死孩子的男人和一个救起孩子的男人，都能"证明"理论，一个用一种机制，另一个用另一种。阿德勒的追随者对任何案例都能这样。

Every event confirmed them. No event could ever refute them. And that, Popper began to suspect, was not strength. It was the giveaway.

每一件事都证实它们，没有任何事能反驳它们。而波普尔开始怀疑，这不是力量，而是破绽。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNExplaining everything explains nothing解释一切等于什么都没解释

Here is the turn. The accepted wisdom of Popper's day said science is distinguished by its empirical method: observe, then infer — induction. Popper said that answer missed the real difference. The real difference was this: a scientific theory is one that can be refuted. He called this property falsifiability: a theory is scientific only if some conceivable observation could prove it wrong.

转折在这里。波普尔那个时代的通行说法是：科学靠的是经验方法——观察，然后归纳。但波普尔说，这个答案错过了真正的差别。真正的差别是：一个科学理论是可以被反驳的。他把这种性质叫做falsifiability（可证伪性）：一个理论是科学的，当且仅当某种可以想象的观察能够证明它是错的。

That flips the usual instinct upside down. We tend to think a good theory is one with lots of confirmations. Popper's first conclusions say the opposite: it is easy to obtain confirmations for nearly every theory, if you look for them. Confirmations only count when they result from risky predictions — predictions that would have refuted the theory if they had come out differently. Easy confirmations are worthless; a genuine test is a serious but unsuccessful attempt to falsify the theory.

这把通常的直觉整个翻了过来。我们总以为好理论就是有很多证实的理论，波普尔的结论却说相反：只要你去找，几乎每个理论都能轻易得到证实。证实只有在来自risky predictions（冒险的预言）时才算数——那些如果结果不同就会反驳理论的预言。轻易的证实一文不值；真正的检验，是一次认真却未能证伪理论的尝试。

And a theory that cannot be refuted by any conceivable event is not scientific. Irrefutability is not a virtue — it is a vice. That is the demarcation criterion: not a test of truth, not a test of meaning, but a line between science and pseudoscience. One claim about what makes a theory scientific is right, and one is the common mistake. Tap a card to see which — and why.

而一个不能被任何可以想象的事件反驳的理论，不是科学。不可反驳不是优点，是缺点。这就是划界标准：不是检验真理，也不是检验意义，而是科学和伪科学之间的分界线。关于什么让理论成为科学，有一个说法是对的，一个是常见的错误。点开卡片，看哪个是、为什么。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTRisky predictions vs. easy confirmations冒险的预言 vs. 轻易的证实

Put the two kinds of theory side by side. Einstein's theory made a prediction so precise that a single expedition could have killed it: the light must bend by a specific amount. That is what a scientific theory looks like — a prohibition. Every good scientific theory is a prohibition: it forbids certain things to happen.

把两种理论并排放在一起。爱因斯坦的理论做了一个精确到一次远征就能杀死它的预言：光必须弯曲一个特定的量。这就是科学理论的样子——一个prohibition（禁令）。每一个好的科学理论都是一条禁令：它禁止某些事情发生。

Marxism and psychoanalysis, as Popper saw them practised, did the opposite. When events seemed to contradict them, their followers did not abandon the theory — they reinterpreted the event, or added a saving assumption, until the theory fit again. A theory rescued this way, Popper argued, loses its scientific status: each rescue makes it explain more and forbid less. The followers' attitude was dogmatic — seek confirmation, resist revision — while science needs the critical attitude: seek tests, admit doubt, and be ready to change your mind.

而马克思主义和精神分析，在波普尔看到的实践中恰恰相反。当事件似乎与它们矛盾时，追随者不是放弃理论，而是重新解释事件、或加一个挽救性假设，直到理论重新合上。波普尔认为，这样被挽救的理论失去了科学地位：每一次挽救都让它解释得更多、禁止得更少。追随者的态度是dogmatic（教条的）——寻求证实、抗拒修正；而科学需要critical attitude（批判的态度）：寻求检验、承认怀疑、随时准备改变主意。

This also changes what "support" means. A theory that survives severe tests has not been proved true — Popper insists no number of confirmations can prove a theory. It has been corroborated: it has stood up to serious attempts to knock it down. Corroboration is not confirmation. Confirmation asks "how much evidence agrees with me?" Corroboration asks "how hard did I try to be wrong — and survive?"

这也改变了"支持"的意思。一个经受住严厉检验的理论并没有被证明为真——波普尔坚持说，再多的证实也证明不了一个理论。它只是被corroborated（确证了）：它挺过了一次次认真的击倒尝试。确证不是证实。证实问的是"有多少证据赞同我？"，确证问的是"我多努力地想证明自己错了——还活了下来？"

So the same word, "confirmed," can mean two opposite things: an easy confirmation that cost the theory nothing, or a risky survival that cost it everything it risked. Only the second one counts.

所以同一个词"被证实"，可以是两种相反的东西：一次没让理论付出任何代价的轻易证实，或一次让它赌上一切的冒险幸存。只有第二种算数。

SCENE 5 · THE RISEScience begins with myths科学始于神话

Where do theories come from? Popper's answer is blunt: there is no recipe. Scientists make them up — bold guesses, conjectures, often born from myths, dreams, and hunches. Science does not begin with careful observations piling up into theories. It begins with a myth, and then with its critical discussion.

理论从哪来？波普尔的回答很直白：没有配方。科学家是编出来的——大胆的猜测、conjectures（猜想），常常来自神话、梦境和直觉。科学不是从小心翼翼的观察堆积成理论开始的，而是从一个神话、然后从对它的critical discussion（批判性讨论）开始的。

That is the method of the whole book, and the meaning of its title: knowledge grows by conjecture and refutation — trial and error. Propose boldly, try hard to show you are wrong, and tentatively keep whatever survives. A theory that survives is not "true forever"; it is the best guess we have, until a better guess and a sharper test come along.

这就是整本书的方法，也是书名的含义：知识靠猜想与反驳生长——试错。大胆提出，努力证明自己错了，暂时保留活下来的东西。活下来的理论不是"永远为真"，而是我们目前最好的猜测，直到更好的猜测和更锋利的检验出现。

So tonight's question has an answer you can carry into every chapter that follows: ask not "what confirms this theory?" but "what would refute it — and has anyone honestly tried?" If nothing could refute it, you are not looking at science. You are looking at something else wearing science's clothes. And the next chapter asks where such questions themselves come from: what is a philosophical problem, and why does it grow out of science?

所以今夜的问题有了一个可以带进后面每一章的答案：不要问"什么证实了这个理论？"，要问"什么能反驳它——有人诚实地试过吗？"如果什么都反驳不了它，你看的就不是科学，而是穿着科学衣服的别的东西。而下一章问的是，这类问题本身从哪来：什么是哲学问题，为什么它生长于科学之中？

TAKEAWAY · 今夜带走三句

  1. A theory is scientific not because it is confirmed, but because it can be refuted — falsifiability is the line between science and pseudoscience.一个理论是科学的，不是因为它被证实，而是因为它能被反驳——可证伪性是科学与伪科学的分界线。
  2. Confirmations count only when they come from risky predictions — easy confirmations are worthless.证实只有来自冒险的预言时才算数——轻易的证实一文不值。
  3. Knowledge grows by conjecture and refutation: bold guesses, severe tests, and whatever survives — tentatively.知识靠猜想与反驳生长：大胆猜测、严厉检验、活下来的东西——暂时保留。

HIGHLIGHTS · 十二句重点亮点

  1. Popper's question since the autumn of 1919: "When should a theory be ranked as scientific?" — not "when is it true?"波普尔自 1919 年秋天起的问题："一个理论什么时候才算科学？"——不是"它什么时候为真？"
  2. Science often errs, and pseudoscience may sometimes stumble on the truth — so truth cannot be the test.科学常常犯错，伪科学有时也会碰巧说对——所以"真"不能是标准。
  3. Four theories filled Vienna in 1919: Einstein's relativity, Marx's theory of history, Freud's psychoanalysis, and Adler's individual psychology.1919 年的维也纳有四个理论：爱因斯坦的相对论、马克思的历史理论、弗洛伊德的精神分析、阿德勒的个体心理学。
  4. Eddington's 1919 eclipse expedition tested Einstein's prediction that light bends near the sun — and the light bent, as predicted.爱丁顿 1919 年的日食远征检验了爱因斯坦的预言：光经过太阳附近会弯曲——光弯曲了，和预言的一样。
  5. What impressed Popper was not that Einstein was proved right, but that he had taken a risk — his theory could have failed, and everyone knew what failure would look like.打动波普尔的不是爱因斯坦被证明是对的，而是他冒了险——他的理论本可能失败，而且每个人都知道失败会是什么样子。
  6. A Marxist saw class struggle in every newspaper; a Freudian could explain any behaviour — every event confirmed them, and no event could refute them.马克思主义者在每份报纸里看到阶级斗争，弗洛伊德主义者能解释任何行为——每一件事都证实它们，没有任何事能反驳它们。
  7. Falsifiability: a theory is scientific only if some conceivable observation could prove it wrong.可证伪性：一个理论是科学的，当且仅当某种可以想象的观察能够证明它是错的。
  8. It is easy to obtain confirmations for nearly every theory, if you look for them — easy confirmations are worthless.只要你去找，几乎每个理论都能轻易得到证实——轻易的证实一文不值。
  9. Every good scientific theory is a prohibition: it forbids certain things to happen.每一个好的科学理论都是一条禁令：它禁止某些事情发生。
  10. A theory rescued by reinterpretation loses scientific status: each rescue makes it explain more and forbid less.靠重新解释挽救的理论会失去科学地位：每一次挽救都让它解释得更多、禁止得更少。
  11. Corroboration is not confirmation: a theory is corroborated when it survives severe attempts to refute it — not when evidence merely agrees with it.确证不是证实：理论在挺过严厉的反驳尝试后才被确证——不是在证据仅仅赞同它时。
  12. Science begins with myths and their critical discussion — bold conjectures, tried hard, kept only tentatively.科学始于神话及其批判性讨论——大胆的猜想，努力检验，暂时保留。

01｜Why did Einstein's theory impress Popper more than Marxism or psychoanalysis?01｜为什么爱因斯坦的理论比马克思主义或精神分析更让波普尔印象深刻？
Because Einstein's theory took a risk: it predicted that light bends near the sun, and Eddington's 1919 eclipse expedition could have refuted it. Marxism and psychoanalysis explained everything, so nothing could ever refute them — and a theory that explains everything forbids nothing.
因为爱因斯坦的理论冒了险：它预言光经过太阳附近会弯曲，爱丁顿 1919 年的日食远征本可以反驳它。马克思主义和精神分析解释一切，所以什么都反驳不了它们——而一个解释一切的理论什么都不禁止。
02｜What is falsifiability, and why is it a criterion of demarcation rather than of truth?02｜什么是可证伪性？为什么它是划界标准，而不是真理标准？
Falsifiability means some conceivable observation could prove the theory wrong. It draws the line between science and pseudoscience — it says nothing about whether the theory is true. Science often errs, and a pseudoscience may stumble on the truth; falsifiability only asks whether the theory takes a testable risk.
可证伪性是指，某种可以想象的观察能够证明理论是错的。它划的是科学与伪科学的分界线——对理论是否为真什么都不说。科学常常犯错，伪科学也可能碰巧说对；可证伪性只问理论是否冒了可检验的风险。
03｜What is the difference between confirmation and corroboration?03｜证实（confirmation）和确证（corroboration）有什么区别？
Confirmation is evidence that agrees with a theory — easy to find for almost any theory, so it counts for little. Corroboration is surviving a severe attempt at refutation: the theory was put at genuine risk and stood up. Only corroboration, from risky predictions, counts as real support.
证实是与理论一致的证据——几乎任何理论都很容易找到，所以没什么分量。确证是挺过一次严厉的反驳尝试：理论被置于真正的风险之中还站住了。只有来自冒险预言的确证，才算真正的支持。
