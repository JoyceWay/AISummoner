NIGHT 01 · 第一夜

CHAPTER 1 · WHY ARE PEOPLE?人为什么存在？

Tonight answers just one question: why are people? The answer starts with Darwin — and ends with the discovery that you are a machine built by something else.

今夜只回答一个问题：人为什么存在？答案从达尔文开始，到一个发现结束：你是一台由别的东西造出来的机器。

SCENE 1 · OPENINGThe question no one could answer没人答得出来的问题

Living things had existed on Earth, without ever knowing why, for over three thousand million years — before the truth finally dawned on one of them. That is how Dawkins opens the book, and it is the boldest opening a biology book can have.

地球上的生物存在了三十多亿年，却从来不知道自己为什么存在——直到真相终于在其中一个身上破晓。这是 Dawkins 开篇的第一句话，也是一本生物学著作能有的最大胆的开场。

Before Darwin, there was no credible answer to the question at all. People had stories — gods, designs, purposes handed down from above. But a story is not an explanation. For thousands of years, the deepest question a mind can ask — why am I here? — had no answer worth the name.

在达尔文之前，这个问题根本没有可信的答案。人们有故事——神、设计、从上面交代下来的目的。但故事不是解释。几千年来，头脑能问的最深的问题——我为什么在这里？——没有一个像样的答案。

Dawkins says intelligent life on a planet comes of age when it first works out the reason for its own existence. That moment arrived in 1859, when Charles Darwin published his theory of evolution. It is, Dawkins argues, the one idea that makes everything else in biology fall into place.

Dawkins 说，一颗行星上的智慧生命，在第一次弄清自己存在的理由时，才算成年。这一刻发生在 1859 年，查尔斯·达尔文发表进化论的那一年。在 Dawkins 看来，这是一个让生物学的一切都各归其位的想法。

So tonight's question is not a small one. It is the question the whole book exists to answer — and the answer is stranger than any of the old stories.

所以今夜的问题不是小问题，它就是整本书为之而写的问题——而答案比所有古老的故事都更奇怪。

SCENE 2 · THE STORYDarwin's answer达尔文的答案

Look at a living thing — a bird's wing, a human eye, a flower that opens at dawn. Everything looks designed, as if someone planned it. Before Darwin, the only explanation for that appearance of design was a designer.

看一个生物——鸟的翅膀、人的眼睛、黎明时分开放的花。一切看起来都像是被设计过的，好像有人规划过。在达尔文之前，对这种"看起来像设计"的唯一解释，就是有个设计者。

Darwin's answer removed the designer and kept the design. Natural selection — the slow, blind sorting of small differences over millions of years — can build the appearance of purpose without any purpose at all. Wings were not drawn on a blueprint. They were shaped, generation after generation, by which birds survived to have more chicks.

达尔文的答案拿掉了设计者，留下了设计。自然选择——几百万年里对微小差异缓慢而盲目的筛选——可以在没有任何目的的情况下，造出"看起来有目的"的东西。翅膀不是按蓝图画出来的，而是一代一代、由哪些鸟活下来生下更多雏鸟塑造出来的。

That is why Dawkins says doubting evolution today is like doubting that the Earth goes around the sun. The evidence is that solid. But the book is not about proving evolution. It is about what evolution means for one thing in particular: the biology of selfishness and altruism — loving, hating, fighting, sharing, stealing, giving.

这就是为什么 Dawkins 说，今天怀疑进化论，就像怀疑地球绕着太阳转。证据就是这么硬。但这本书不是为了证明进化论，它要讲的是进化论对一件事意味着什么：自私与利他的生物学——爱、恨、争斗、分享、偷窃、给予。

And here is the twist that makes the book famous: to understand selfishness and altruism, Dawkins argues, you must stop looking at the animal — and start looking at the gene.

而让这本书出名的转折在这里：要理解自私与利他，Dawkins 说，你必须停止看动物——开始看基因。

SCENE 3 · THE TURNThe Chicago gangsters芝加哥黑帮

Suppose you are told that a man lived a long and prosperous life in the world of 1920s Chicago gangsters. You know nothing else about him — but you can already guess something about his character. Tough. Quick on the trigger. Good at keeping loyal friends. The guesses are not certain, but they are reasonable: the world he survived in selects for exactly those qualities.

假设有人告诉你，一个人在 1920 年代芝加哥黑帮的世界里活得又长又风光。关于他，你别的什么都不知道——但你已经能猜出他的性格。强硬。手快。善于留住忠心的朋友。这些猜测不一定对，但很合理：他活下来的那个世界，恰恰会筛选出这些品质。

Now apply the same logic to genes. Our genes have survived, in some cases for millions of years, in a highly competitive world. Like successful Chicago gangsters, they have prospered where others died out. So what quality should we expect in them?

现在把同样的逻辑用到基因上。我们的基因在竞争激烈的世界里活了下来，有些已经活了几百万年。像成功的芝加哥黑帮一样，它们在别人死掉的地方繁荣起来。那么，我们应该期待它们有什么品质？

Like successful Chicago gangsters, our genes have survived, in some cases for millions of years, in a highly competitive world.
像成功的芝加哥黑帮一样，我们的基因在竞争激烈的世界里活了下来，有些已经活了几百万年。

Dawkins argues the answer is ruthless selfishness — a gene that does not look after its own copying gets copied less, and disappears. A gene that promotes its own survival, at whatever cost to the body carrying it, spreads. That is the logic of the whole book, stated in one paragraph.

Dawkins 说，答案是ruthless selfishness（无情的自私）——一个不照顾自己复制的基因，被复制得越来越少，最后消失；而一个促进自身生存的基因，不管对携带它的身体付出什么代价，都会扩散开来。这就是整本书的逻辑，写在一段话里。

But wait — "selfish" here does not mean what you think it means. That is the trap the title sets, and the next scene springs it. Tap a card to see which claim about "selfish" is right.

但等一下——这里的"自私"不是你以为的那个意思。这是书名设下的陷阱，下一幕就拆穿它。点开卡片，看关于"自私"的哪个说法是对的。

SCENE 4 · THE CONTRASTThe group is not the hero群体不是主角

Before Dawkins, the popular story of evolution went like this: animals behave for the good of the species. Birds limit their own breeding so the species doesn't starve; animals sacrifice themselves so the group survives. Writers like Konrad Lorenz built whole books on that idea.

在 Dawkins 之前，进化论的流行故事是这样的：动物是为了物种的好处而行动的。鸟儿限制自己的繁殖，免得物种饿死；动物牺牲自己，好让群体活下去。康拉德·洛伦兹这样的作家整本书都建立在这个想法上。

Dawkins says that story is wrong. It is not the species that survives and reproduces — species don't have children. Individuals do. And it is not really the individual either, because the individual dies. What survives, generation after generation, is the gene: copied from body to body, while bodies come and go.

Dawkins 说这个故事是错的。活下来并繁殖的不是物种——物种不会生孩子，是个体在生。而严格说也不是个体，因为个体会死。一代一代活下来的，是基因：从一个身体复制到另一个身体，而身体来了又去。

So when an animal seems to sacrifice itself for the group, the gene's-eye view asks a different question: does that sacrifice help copies of the same gene sitting in other bodies? A mother bird risking her life for her chicks is not being noble for the species — she is protecting copies of her own genes, carried in her young.

所以当一只动物似乎为群体牺牲自己时，基因视角会问一个不同的问题：这种牺牲有没有帮助坐在别的身体里的、相同的基因拷贝？一只母鸟为雏鸟冒生命危险，不是在为物种高尚——她是在保护自己基因的拷贝，装在她的孩子身体里。

That one shift — from the group, to the individual, to the gene — dissolves the puzzle of altruism. Apparent selflessness and apparent selfishness can both be explained by the same thing: gene selfishness. The rest of the book is the proof, case by case.

这一次转换——从群体，到个体，到基因——解开了利他之谜。表面上的无私和表面上的自私，都可以用同一样东西解释：基因的自私。整本书的其余部分，就是一个案例一个案例地证明这一点。

SCENE 5 · THE RISESurvival machines生存机器

If genes are the survivors, what are we? Dawkins gives the answer in the book's most famous image:

如果基因是幸存者，那我们是什么？Dawkins 用这本书最著名的意象给出答案：

We are survival machines — robot vehicles blindly programmed to preserve the selfish molecules known as genes.
我们是生存机器——被盲目编程、用来保存那些叫作基因的自私分子的机器人载具。

A gene leaps from body to body down the generations, manipulating body after body in its own way and for its own ends, abandoning a succession of bodies before they sink in senility and death. The genes are the immortals. The bodies are the throwaway machines they build, use, and discard.

一个基因一代一代地从一个身体跳到另一个身体，用自己的方式、为自己的目的操纵一个又一个身体，在它们衰老死亡之前抛弃它们。基因是不朽的，身体是它们造出来、用完就扔的一次性机器。

It is a dizzying reversal of how we usually see ourselves. We think of genes as small parts of us. Dawkins says it is the other way round: we are large parts of them — their vehicles, their survival machines.

这是对我们通常看自己的方式的一次眩晕的翻转。我们以为基因是我们身体里的小零件，Dawkins 说反了：我们是它们的大零件——它们的载具，它们的生存机器。

And yet the chapter ends with hope, not despair. Dawkins holds out the possibility that our species alone on Earth has the power to rebel against the designs of the selfish gene — to teach generosity, to choose altruism, precisely because we can understand what our genes are doing to us. Understanding is the first step of the rebellion.

然而这一章的结尾是希望，不是绝望。Dawkins 提出，地球上只有我们这个物种有能力反抗自私基因的设计——教慷慨、选择利他，正因为我们能理解基因在对我们做什么。理解，是反抗的第一步。

But to rebel, you first need to know what you are rebelling against. What is a gene, really — and how did the first ones arise? That is the next night's story, and it begins in a soup.

但要反抗，你首先得知道自己在反抗什么。基因到底是什么——最初的基因又是怎么来的？那是下一夜的故事，而它开始于一锅汤。

它指的是基因对生存的影响：一个基因如果帮助自己的拷贝扩散，就是"自私"的。它不是指人的感受、动机或道德——更不是自私的许可证。Dawkins 说我们应该教慷慨，正因为我们生来是自私的。
02｜Why is the "good of the species" the wrong level to explain behaviour?02｜为什么"为了物种的好处"是解释行为的错误层面？
Species don't reproduce — individuals do, and individuals die. What survives generation after generation is the gene, copied from body to body. So behaviour is best explained by what it does for the gene's copying, not for the species.
物种不会繁殖——个体会，而个体会死。一代一代活下来的，是从一个身体复制到另一个身体的基因。所以行为最好用它对基因复制的作用来解释，而不是用它对物种的作用。
03｜What is a "survival machine" — and who is the immortal in this story?03｜什么是"生存机器"——这个故事里的不朽者是谁？
You are the survival machine: a body built and programmed by genes to keep them alive. The genes are the immortals — they leap from body to body down the generations, abandoning each body before it dies. The machine is throwaway; the gene goes on.
你就是生存机器：一个由基因造出来、被编程来让它们活下去的身体。基因是不朽者——它们一代一代从一个身体跳到另一个身体，在每个身体死之前抛弃它。机器是一次性的，基因一直传下去。
